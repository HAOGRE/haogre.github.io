_The one he did not get appraised was genuine._

The first time Steve brought a strange object back to the village, he thought he had found treasure.

It had been wedged in iron ore, too filthy to identify. He handed it to the smith. The smith took one look and threw it back.

Steve ran to the river and washed his hair thirty times.

Later, he found something resembling an End crystal. This custom world really did contain such a material. His initial excitement was understandable.

He brought it in for appraisal too.

The smith examined it and tossed it back onto him.

It was old filth. It was not a crystal at all.

This time, Steve soaked in the river for thirty minutes. The first wash had been measured in repetitions; the second in time. Both produced the same result: no treasure, and he had to clean himself first.

The next day, he found another piece in a stronghold jail.

Steve looked at it without getting excited.

“Again?”

He dealt with it decisively this time. He took it to lava and threw it in.

It sank and disappeared.

Back in the village, he told the smith. The smith's first question was, “Where did you find it?”

“The stronghold jail.”

The smith's expression changed.

“That was a real End crystal!”

Steve was quiet for a moment.

He had carefully brought home both fakes. The real thing had spared the smith a visit.

Later, while heading out for supplies, he found a totem of undying along the way.

Steve stood holding it for a moment.

This time, he did not head toward the lava.

He returned to the village and handed it to the smith. The smith recognized it and quickly pushed it back into his hand.

“It can save you from one fatal hit. Keep it.”

Steve tightened his grip on the totem and finally smiled.

This time, the useful object was still there.

And he did not have to get into the river again.
