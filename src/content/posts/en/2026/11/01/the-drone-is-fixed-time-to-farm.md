It was a fresh save, with mods enabled. The first thing Steve, Alex, and Pangpang did after spawning was find a village and buy a house.

Pangpang reached up and touched the roof as he stepped inside.

“This one already has a roof.”

Steve set down a crafting table. Alex went to learn her way around. A little later, she flew past the window sideways.

The three of them soon learned the village layout: the well was in the middle, the blacksmith was to the east, and Alex usually landed to the west.

She and the iron golem never quite got along.

“Could you walk around it?” Steve asked.

“I do.” Alex rubbed her shoulder. “It sends me back in a straight line.”

Then the pillagers came.

Their captain had barely stepped off the dirt path beside the fields, banner held high, when the iron golem met him. One swing sent both captain and banner over the fence.

Alex stood in the doorway, watching very closely.

“So that's what it looks like from over here.”

Pangpang had been about to shut the door. His hand paused.

“Enough research. The crossbows are still out there.”

But Steve was rummaging through his inventory.

He pulled out a small gray aircraft with four square rotor arms and a body wider than the tabletop. Neither of the others knew where he had got it.

“Military drone,” he said.

Pangpang took half a step back.

“Does military equipment have to leave through our front door too?”

Steve carried it outside and crouched down to set it straight. The rotors spun, kicking up a ring of dust. The drone wobbled, lifted, and climbed above the fence.

Steve was just about to speak when a crossbow twanged beyond the village.

A bolt stuck in the body. Another struck a moment later. The steady hum turned into a ragged series of hiccups.

The drone tipped sideways and dropped into the dirt beside the road.

Between takeoff and landing, Pangpang had not even found a good place to watch.

“Military,” Alex said.

Steve lifted the bent rotor arm.

“I heard you.”

Once things were quiet at the village entrance, they carried the drone to the blacksmith.

The blacksmith looked at the machine, then at Steve. He handed over a few iron nails and a pinch of redstone.

Pangpang held the materials and waited.

“Is that all?”

The blacksmith spread his hands.

“You came for materials, didn't you?”

They put the drone on some wooden boards outside the house. Steve did the repairs, Alex held the crooked frame steady, and Pangpang found the nails whenever they fell.

Of the three jobs, Pangpang's involved the most walking.

Nails slipped through gaps in the boards. Redstone smeared a sleeve. As soon as they straightened one side, the other sagged. Steve tried the rotors. All three leaned down and listened to it clicking in place.

Pangpang brought over a chair.

“For me?” Steve asked.

“For me. I'll have to crawl underneath again in a minute.”

After a long time, the drone finally made an even humming sound again.

This time it rose steadily above the clearing, hovered, and settled back onto the boards. It did not tip, fall apart, or shake loose the nails they had just retrieved.

Steve let go, his shoulders dropping.

“Done.”

Alex glanced at the sky, then at the empty ground behind the house.

“Time to farm, then.”

Steve looked up at her.

She handed him a hoe.

“We bought a house. We still have to eat.”

_The rotors had stopped. The hoes hadn't._

They opened up a few rows beside the water. Steve tilled the soil, Alex worked along the next row, and Pangpang followed, placing the seeds one at a time.

They had brought the drone along too. It sat neatly on its boards at the edge of the field.

When Pangpang reached it, he looked up.

“Should we get it to help?”

Steve's hoe stopped.

All three looked at the machine they had worked so hard to repair.

“Let's leave it there for now,” Steve said.

Pangpang nodded and went back to planting.

The iron golem passed along the dirt path. Alex moved herself and her hoe one space farther into the field. Just then, Pangpang planted the last seed.

They had a house. The field was planted.

Steve picked up the drone and carried it home.

Pangpang followed. “Flying it again tomorrow?”

Steve set it down gently inside and shut the door first.

“We'll check the field first.”
