_The weapons could wait. This row was not finished._

After the player with their stolen diamond sword had run off, a patrol approached from the distance.

Thirty guards, all in untrimmed netherite armor. Their leader rode a black horse wearing diamond horse armor. His own netherite armor had distinctive diamond trim.

Steve and Alex watched them pursue the sword thief. That equipment would be difficult to mistake.

Later, the two chopped some wood and followed the direction the patrol had taken.

The first thing they found was the black horse.

It stood beside the road. The patrol was absent.

Steve took its reins and mounted. Alex climbed up behind him. Farther on, they found a small brown horse for her to ride.

She looked at her mount, then at Steve's.

“Yours is still bigger.”

Before he could answer, a backpack appeared in the grass ahead.

Then more scattered belongings.

They slowed and recognized members of the patrol. The orderly column they had seen earlier was silent now. The leader was not among them.

Each put on a set of untrimmed netherite armor, gathered weapons and supplies, and continued searching.

Eventually, they found the leader.

He had fallen too. His distinctive equipment had been taken. There was nothing left nearby to collect.

Steve looked at the black horse, then at the empty place. The mount remained; its rider did not.

They could only continue.

A bank of fog lay ahead. They passed through it into an ordinary landscape: grass, trees, and a village in the distance.

Steve pulled up his horse. At last, the things they had seen along the road fitted together.

“They were poisoned.”

That did not tell him who had done it. The road beyond the fog continued, and so did the questions.

At the village, one of those questions suddenly stood in front of them in a different form.

Someone was wearing the leader's trimmed netherite armor and attacking villagers. Even the iron golem had failed to stop him. He was eating cookies to restore his health.

Steve and Alex recognized the armor.

They rushed in and defeated him.

As the village entrance fell quiet, Alex kept looking at the equipment.

“Who was he?”

Steve did not know. Wearing the leader's belongings did not answer every question from the road. But the attacker could no longer use them to chase villagers.

Later, the villagers gave the two a small house.

Steve had started by talking about buying it. By the end of the conversation, he realized it was a gift.

There was land outside. They put away their belongings, picked up tools, and began planting.

Till, sow, harvest, plant again. Steve finished one row; Alex took the next. Once one end was dealt with, the other end had something waiting.

Their weapons were set aside. Their hoes became increasingly familiar.

Villagers sometimes passed the fields. Later, a few brought them ender pearls.

Sixteen for Steve. Sixteen for Alex.

They each counted their share, put it away, and went back to the field.

Alex rested on her tool and glanced toward the house. The black horse was still there.

“Are we leaving today?”

Steve looked at the unfinished row.

“Let's finish this part first.”

So they planted another row.
