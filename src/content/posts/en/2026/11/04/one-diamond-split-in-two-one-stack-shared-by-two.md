_Two shares were ready. There was no third._

The three spawned on the grass in a new save.

Steve was the first to get a diamond.

Alex saw it. Pangpang saw it too. Steve looked at them, pulled his hand toward his chest, and began running before they had discussed anything.

One diamond, three people. They had not agreed on a way to share it, but the pursuit was unanimous.

After a while, he finally stopped. Diamonds in this custom world could actually be broken apart. He split the single gem in two: half for Alex, half for Pangpang.

They accepted their pieces.

Steve withdrew his hands. The diamond business seemed finished.

Until he checked his backpack again.

There was a whole stack inside.

Sixty-four diamonds.

Steve closed the bag. None of the willingness he had shown while sharing half a diamond seemed to remain.

“I'm not sharing these.”

Alex looked at Pangpang.

Pangpang looked at Steve.

Steve ran again.

This time, sixty-four diamonds bounced along in his pack. After sharing the first gem, he felt he had already given up plenty. With a whole stack, every possible share suddenly seemed too large.

He was still muttering about an earlier idea of “fifteen each.” That calculation never became a distribution. His feet merely got faster.

Eventually, he stopped again.

Alex and Pangpang took out the stack and divided it into two portions.

Thirty-two.

Thirty-two.

Steve watched them put their shares away, then reached toward the middle.

There was no third portion.

A manure prank followed, leaving him with a mouthful he hurried to rinse out. For the moment, he could not voice any of the distributions he had wanted to argue about.

With one diamond, the other two had each received half.

With one stack, the other two had each received half a stack.

Standing by the water, Steve realized that neither arrangement had included him.

---

When the three found a village, Steve received a gift that was difficult to overlook.

A diamond block.

Square and brilliantly blue. Holding it, he glanced at Alex, then at Pangpang.

Then he discovered that it could be unpacked.

Nine diamonds.

Steve swept all nine toward himself. He moved quickly, as though putting them away promptly would settle the distribution.

Alex came over.

Pangpang came over too.

“Are they all with you?”

Steve looked down. All nine were present. His problem was not finding his belongings. It was that other people found them too accurately.

They reached over.

Alex took four diamonds. Pangpang took four as well.

Steve checked his side again.

One.

When he had gathered all nine, he plainly had not expected the final count to be this quick.

Before he could argue further, a lump of manure landed on his head.

Steve held his neck stiffly, gripping the single diamond.

He had indeed accepted the gift himself.

He had indeed unpacked the block himself.

As for how much he could keep afterward, two other people had just decided that.

He put the last diamond away and freed his hands to deal with his head.

Nine minus four, minus another four.

This time, at least, he did not need to search his backpack to confirm the result.
