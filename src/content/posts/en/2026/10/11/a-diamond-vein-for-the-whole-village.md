Steve had been following the diamond vein for a long time. His torches were nearly gone, and there was still no end in sight.

He turned and called for Alex.

His voice echoed twice through the cave.

Pangpang answered first. “We’re still going?”

Steve pointed at the wall.

Blue flecks stretched away into the darkness.

Pangpang glanced at the iron pickaxe in his hand.

“This might not be enough.”

## A Thousand Blocks Is Just the Length

This world had mods for enormous ore veins and villagers who could work. They knew there would be plenty of ore. They had never found so much that they needed to go back and fetch the village.

Alex wrote down the coordinates of the starting point and the farthest point they had measured, then estimated the length of the vein.

Nearly a thousand blocks.

Pangpang immediately started calculating how many diamonds that meant.

Alex put her notebook away.

“That’s the length. We haven’t started counting the ore yet.”

Steve was already heading back.

“Count later. Let’s get more pickaxes.”

The first thing they brought back to the village wasn’t diamonds. It was a very long map of the route.

The blacksmith spread it across the table. Halfway through, he ran out of table. The farmer moved a basket, and another section unfolded. Pangpang ended up standing at the edge, holding the part that nearly reached the floor.

The villagers gathered around.

One of them had only agreed to lend two iron pickaxes. After seeing the whole map, he went home and brought the third one too.

## First, Make Room to Move

The next day, the whole village arrived at the mine entrance.

Some brought pickaxes, some brought planks, and some brought bread. Pangpang checked the supply chest, found plenty of bread, and felt very pleased.

Once underground, he felt less pleased.

The tunnel was too narrow.

The people at the front were digging, but the people behind them couldn’t get in. Anyone trying to leave with a full load of ore was blocked by someone bringing torches. Steve stood in the middle with his pickaxe raised while people at both ends asked him to move over.

He was already pressed against the wall.

Alex called a halt.

They widened the section near the entrance and dug alcoves for chests. Ore went out along one side; people coming in used the other. Torches and arrows marked the junctions. Then they laid minecart tracks with room for the carts to turn around, passing full chests along toward the exit.

Pangpang was in charge of recording which section each chest came from.

He had thought this job wouldn’t involve digging.

But the ground under the chests was uneven, so he had to dig anyway.

It was noon before the first cartload of diamonds finally emerged from the mine.

A villager rang the bell by the entrance once.

Pangpang poked his head out and looked at the sun.

“We spent most of the morning building roads.”

Alex passed him, pushing the next cart.

“Without them, the first cart would still be stuck inside.”

## Pangpang Drops Down Another Level

Traffic moved much more smoothly that afternoon.

Steve pushed ahead with a few villagers, Alex checked the branching tunnels against her map, and Pangpang delivered an empty chest to a bend.

He thought there was still a solid block of stone behind him.

There was only a thin layer of loose rubble.

Pangpang sat back, and that small patch of floor dropped away beneath him.

It wasn’t a long fall.

But it was enough for him to land squarely on his backside.

“Stop the carts!” he shouted.

Steve put down his pickaxe and ran over. Alex lowered a torch into the hole.

Pangpang sat at the bottom with his back to them. Suddenly, he went very still.

“Are you hurt?” Steve asked.

“Take a look down here first.”

Where the rubble had slipped away, a broad patch of blue flecks was exposed. They had been following the upper vein all along. There was another branch underneath. It ran off to one side along the stone wall, still gleaming in the distance.

Steve stared for a long time.

Alex opened her notebook.

Pangpang raised a hand.

“Before you write that down, pull me out.”

## Closing In on Three Thousand

They didn’t send the whole village crowding into the new hole at once.

First they built steps, checked for cavities in the surrounding rock, and extended the hauling route off to the side. Once Pangpang climbed out, the first thing he did was put a fence around the opening.

The second was to get a piece of bread.

Only then did he ask whether he could take credit for the discovery.

Steve said yes.

Pangpang immediately proposed that he should never have to carry chests again.

Alex said no.

Over the next few days, chests began to pile up at the village entrance. Every load was logged when it came out. Moving it again didn’t add to the count, and anything still in the mine wasn’t counted in advance.

The final tally came to nearly three thousand diamonds.

When Pangpang heard the number, he immediately looked at his iron pickaxe.

The one he had first taken down into the mine had broken long ago.

The blacksmith sorted out new tools for the villagers at the crafting table. Steve finally made himself a full set of diamond gear. Alex folded up the map. It was much thicker than it had been at the start.

Pangpang stood in the doorway, holding the last empty chest.

“Are we going back?”

Steve pointed at the chest. “Let’s get this lot put away first.”

Pangpang set it down and, out of habit, started to sit on it. Alex had barely opened her mouth when he straightened up on his own.

“I know,” he said. “Check underneath first.”
