At the entrance to the eighth floor, Pangpang drank his potion with great care.

The sign had a short message.

Three Lynels in a row.

“There was only one last time,” he said.

Steve nodded.

“We only just stocked back up.”

Steve nodded again.

Pangpang checked that all five Savage Lynel Bows were there before stepping through the portal.

## Three Could Be Reasoned With

Deep inside the eighth floor of this modded dungeon, three cages stood in a row.

The first held a White-Maned Lynel. Steve dodged, seized his opening, and leaped onto its back. His teammates followed up with attacks. Its health bar emptied before it could finish its big move.

Pangpang felt a little better.

The second, a Silver Lynel, shook the ground so hard that all five of them backed up several steps. Pangpang’s spirits retreated with them.

The third was a fire Lynel.

The moment its cage opened, half the arena heated up.

They couldn’t stay close for long. They took turns firing their Savage Lynel Bows from a distance, waiting for the flames to die down so they could squeeze in another attack.

When all three cages were empty, Pangpang rested his shield against his leg and looked at the door across the arena.

“Surely we get a break on the ninth floor.”

Alex said nothing to that.

First, she counted their remaining potions.

## Five Were Less Open to Reason

The ninth-floor notice promised five fights in a row.

The first four were fire, ice, lightning, and water.

The five of them gritted their teeth and fought through. Only then did the last stone platform open.

Out came the Lynel Tyrant.

It didn’t wait for Pangpang to finish reading its name.

Explosions struck the arena. The first broke their formation; the second followed right behind. Everything flashed white, and all five were looking at the entrance again.

Pangpang sat down.

“This one saved us all the time the first four took.”

On their second attempt, they saved more potions and finally reached the Tyrant again.

This time it was Planetary Devastation, followed by fireballs blanketing the area.

All five were back at the entrance to the eighth floor.

Steve looked up to check where they were. He said nothing.

On the third attempt, they didn’t even see the Tyrant.

A message appeared in midair: after more than two failures, the first four Lynels would enter together.

Four elements closed in from four directions.

Before Pangpang could decide whether to dodge the fire or the lightning first, he was already back.

He stood at the entrance for a while.

“The reward for failing is a harder fight?”

Deep Valley Miner nodded.

“This level holds a grudge.”

## Enough Shields, Not Enough Hands

They stocked up on an enormous pile of shields.

Pangpang stuffed his backpack full and patted the chest. “Hundreds of millions. That’ll do.”

Alex glanced over.

“Never mind counting them. You need to equip them in time.”

On their fourth attempt, they really did last longer.

When a shield broke, they swapped it out. A teammate would cover while someone took half a step back. They finally cleared the first four, then chipped away at the Tyrant’s health, a little at a time.

Everyone watched the shrinking sliver of health.

The Tyrant suddenly closed in.

A point-blank impact knocked Pangpang’s shield aside. A spinning greatsword swept in after it.

Pangpang reached for his next shield.

Before he could pull it out, the fight was over.

Back at the entrance, he opened his backpack.

There were still plenty of spare shields.

Every last one was there.

“Now we know,” he said. “It wasn’t a shortage of shields.”

## Three More Upstairs

Steve switched to the Inkcrystal Sword.

It was a piece of test gear they had picked up earlier, and its flight ability still worked. After a brief discussion, they decided to fly past and see what lay ahead.

They skirted the combat area and reached the entrance to the tenth floor.

Three Tyrants stood inside.

Steve grabbed Pangpang. Alex grabbed Steve. Almost without stopping, all five retraced their steps to the ninth floor.

Pangpang leaned against the wall, catching his breath.

“The last one suddenly seems quite polite.”

Ender Hunter reopened the stats panel.

Every stat was maxed out.

After looking, they all lowered their weapons a little.

“Let’s check the map first,” Alex said.

## The Side Path Had Been There All Along

At last, they made a circuit of the arena’s edge.

Deep Valley Miner was the first to spot a corner off to one side. Around it was another small sign on the wall. It explained that the difficult challenges were optional, and that a side path led to the reward area.

Steve read it, then read it again.

Pangpang peered over his shoulder.

“When did they put that up?”

Nobody knew.

On their earlier attempts, they had only had eyes for the Lynels.

The five of them followed the side path, bypassing the ninth floor, then the tenth. The crashes behind them faded into the distance. Three chests appeared ahead.

No fourth Lynel jumped out.

Pangpang waited a few seconds before approaching.

The first chest held a divine sword called the Sword of the Gullible Demon King.

The listed damage was high: four hits would bring down a Lynel Tyrant with normal stats. As for the versions with maxed-out stats on these two floors, the five exchanged a look. They had no immediate plans to go back and test it.

The other two chests were empty.

Pangpang closed the second, opened the third, then went back to check the second again.

Steve waited for him.

“No change,” Pangpang said.

A player who had just arrived nearby brought another piece of news that made setting out again even less appealing: the admin had obtained a staff that could summon Tyrants.

The five looked at one another.

Pangpang packed the divine sword away first.

“How many can he summon at once?”

The player wasn’t sure.

Steve put away the map. “Back to the surface. Keep gearing up.”

This time, nobody objected.

On the way back, Pangpang read every sign carefully. At each fork, he made a point of looking off to the side.

Ender Hunter asked what he was looking for.

“A way through.”

Pangpang paused.

“This time, I’m looking first.”
