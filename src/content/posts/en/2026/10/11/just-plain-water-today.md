The first time the drinksmith treated them to ice-cold cola, Steve had only three segments left on his thirst bar.

One bottle, and it was full.

A cooling icon appeared beside it, too.

Pangpang looked at the icon and asked if he could have another bottle.

The drinksmith turned around and flipped over his price board.

The first one was on the house. After that, he was open for business.

## Sixteen Slots Left in the Fridge

This world had a hydration mod installed, and the drinksmith was a new villager profession. Plain water restored thirst; cold drinks also gave a brief status effect. None of the three had seen this before. They packed a few bottles and headed into the woods.

When they got back, the cola was still there. The chill wasn't.

Steve turned a bottle over, looking for an explanation. Alex opened the item description. Beside them, Pangpang drank the last mouthful.

“It's at room temperature now,” Alex said.

“Still drinkable,” Pangpang said.

“The effect's gone.”

Pangpang thought for a moment. “Then next time, drink faster.”

The drinksmith had a more expensive solution. They bought cooling charms, and the carpenter delivered a large fridge with room for sixty-four bottles.

They each put in sixteen bottles, neatly filling forty-eight slots. With another four bottles in each person's charm, they had twenty apiece.

Pangpang stared at the sixteen empty slots.

“We don't have to fill it,” Alex said at once.

“Seems a shame to leave them empty.”

“Running out of money would be more of a shame.”

She worked out the day's expenses for the other two. By the time Steve reached the bottom, he had put back the bottle he'd just taken out.

Pangpang put one back, too.

It hadn't been opened. He made a point of saying that twice.

The next day, there was plain drinking water on the table. The cold cola was saved for long trips, or when they actually needed the status effect.

The arrangement lasted only a few days.

## Suddenly, Three Tons in Storage

A crate dropped at the entrance to the village.

Strange things fell onto this server fairly often, so the villagers kept well back. Steve approached with a shield. Alex circled around to the side. Pangpang stood where he could see the lid.

The crate opened. Nothing exploded.

Inside was cola.

Three whole tons.

First Pangpang grinned. Then he went to look at their fridge.

The fridge still had sixty-four slots.

The arrival of three tons of cola had not made it any bigger.

They hauled it home from the village entrance. By the third trip, there was nowhere left to put their feet. Steve came through the door carrying a box and couldn't reach the floor. He had to step onto another box instead.

“Stop putting it in the bedroom,” Alex said.

Pangpang sat on the doorstep, holding their old expense sheet.

“It's cheap now.”

Steve stood in the hallway with a box in his arms.

“Space got expensive.”

## Free Things Expire Too

The drinksmith came to help check the stock. In one box, he found dates on the bottoms of the bottles.

This mod had expiration dates.

The three leaned in to look for a while, then turned together toward the boxes filling the house.

“I could drink a little more each day,” Pangpang said.

Alex opened her notebook.

“Tell me how much per day. Then I'll tell you how many days it'll take.”

Pangpang didn't give her a number.

They moved the bottles closest to expiring to the front. Unopened bottles that were still in date went to nearby villages in batches. Steve found a handcart, Alex kept the records, and Pangpang packed the boxes.

One villager wanted a few extra boxes. Pangpang looked at the free space in the man's house and earnestly suggested measuring it first.

The villager said he had a big house.

Pangpang suggested he come and look at the bedroom back at their place first.

In the end, the villager wheeled away just one cartload.

After those busy few days, they could finally open the fridge door properly again. They had kept only a small supply of cold drinks for themselves, with plenty of drinking water stored nearby. The dates were written on a wooden sign. Older bottles in front, newer ones behind.

Steve raised his cup and took a long drink.

Pangpang looked at him. “That's water.”

“I know.”

“We've got plenty of cola now.”

“I know that, too.”

Alex closed her notebook and poured herself a cup of plain water. Pangpang thought for a moment, then fetched a third cup.

No one opened the fridge.

The next day, the drinksmith came by to ask if they needed a restock.

Pangpang turned the sign by the door toward him.

On it was a short message Steve had just written.

> Just plain water today.
