When the copper chest opened, Pangpang was upstairs pressing the glowstone for the second time.

Steve and Alex had spent ages trying to open it downstairs. They'd struck it with a pickaxe, pushed at it with a sword hilt, and even gone around the back looking for a lock. Some corrosion flaked off. The lid didn't budge.

Pangpang watched for a while, then remembered that touching the glowstone upstairs was how they'd found this little underground room in the first place.

“I'll be right back,” he said.

Steve held up his pickaxe. “What are you getting?”

“Nothing yet.”

A moment later, a soft click came from inside the wall.

The lid sprang open by itself.

Pangpang ran down the steps, looked at the chest, then at Steve's pickaxe.

“Why are you still holding that up?”

“In case it changes its mind.”

There was only one thing in the chest: a name tag.

On it was a set of coordinates.

Alex worked out the distance, turned the tag over, and worked it out again.

“Three hundred thousand blocks.”

Steve lowered his pickaxe.

Pangpang wanted to close the chest again.

## Three Hundred Thousand Blocks Away, Just a Flower

They'd originally come looking for the server owner, who was being held captive.

But the obsidian cage inside the statue was empty. A button beside it led to a waterway, and the waterway led to an empty meeting room. Touching the glowstone there opened yet another passage beneath their feet.

After all that, they'd finally found something they could take with them.

And it was telling them to travel another three hundred thousand blocks.

“Whoever built all these contraptions,” Pangpang said, “do they just not like visitors?”

Alex put the name tag away. “Let's head back to the hub first.”

A few players at the hub knew how to build ender pearl cannons. They checked the coordinates, spent a while putting one together, then set a bucket of water beside it and told Steve to get into position first.

Pangpang stared in the direction they would be launched and checked his inventory twice.

“Is this thing accurate?”

One player pointed to a distant crater left by a test.

Pangpang asked no more questions.

By the time all three were back on solid ground, they were just over three hundred blocks from their destination. Steve brought up the coordinates. Alex checked the direction. Pangpang checked the bread.

They walked the last stretch.

At the destination, there was no fortress, no prison, and no captive server owner.

Just a flower.

It was an ordinary flower. It swayed a little in the grass.

Pangpang walked a circle around it.

“Three hundred thousand blocks,” he reminded Steve.

Steve nodded.

“Just this.”

Steve nodded again.

Alex crouched down and spotted a suspiciously neat seam beside the flower's roots. All three crowded onto the block where it stood, and she reached out and picked it.

The ground around them suddenly dropped.

Dirt, turf, and the stone slabs hidden underneath withdrew layer by layer, leaving only the block beneath their feet. Below, a staircase appeared, leading underground.

Pangpang gripped Steve's arm and didn't let go.

“Now the flower's starting to make the trip worthwhile.”

## Three Hundred, Every Time

In the first stone room, they found a second name tag.

It held another set of coordinates, with MINECRAFT written beside them.

Alex compared them with their current position.

Three hundred blocks.

They followed the passage and found another stone room.

The same entrance. The same stone pedestal. The same name tag.

The next stop was another three hundred blocks away.

Starting with the fourth room, Alex wrote a number beside each doorway. Steve placed all the torches on the left to mark their way back. Pangpang was in charge of checking chests. Unfortunately, there weren't any.

At room twenty, he raised an objection.

“Can I have a different job?”

“Like what?”

“Looking for food.”

Alex thought about it. “Isn't that what you've been doing?”

They kept going.

The room numbers went from two digits to three. They used up several stacks of torches. There were no monsters in the passages, and no arrows came flying out of nowhere. There was little danger and a lot of walking, so much that Pangpang began to miss a bit of ordinary danger.

“At least a creeper wouldn't make me walk this far.”

Steve looked back at him.

“It would make you walk it all again.”

Pangpang took that back.

They stopped to rest a few times. Each time, Alex checked the coordinates. The distance was always three hundred. Not one block less. Not one block more.

In room three hundred, Pangpang sat beside the stone pedestal and ate some bread.

A crumb fell to the floor and slowly rolled into a crack at the base of the wall. He reached to pick it up and felt a faint, cool draft against his fingertips.

“This room's drafty.”

Alex was writing the number beside the doorway. At his words, she glanced at the base of the wall and made a note on her paper.

Steve picked up the next name tag.

Another three hundred blocks.

They went on anyway.

## The Number They Walked Past

At room three hundred and seventeen, Pangpang sat down and refused to move.

Steve set the new name tag in front of him.

Pangpang didn't take it.

“How far?”

“Three hundred.”

“You don't even have to look anymore.”

Alex spread out her notes from the journey. The room numbers kept climbing, the coordinates changed from one stretch to the next, and the number three hundred filled a long column on the page.

“It doesn't look like we're going in circles,” she said. “The position changes every time.”

Steve leaned against the wall. “Then we're just not there yet.”

Pangpang stared at the 317 on the doorway and stopped mid-bite.

“We've been treating three hundred as a distance.”

They both looked at him.

“But what if it means something else, too?”

Pangpang pointed to the room number.

“Like room three hundred.”

Steve opened his inventory and took out the name tag they'd brought from the previous room, then the one from this room. The coordinates on both clearly pointed them toward the next stretch of the route. Neither promised that keeping going would get them to the end.

“That's just a guess,” Alex said.

“So let's go back and look.”

Pangpang paused and rubbed his fingers.

“There was a draft by the wall in that room, too.”

Alex looked through her notes. Her finger stopped beside 300.

Sure enough, she'd written one small word there: “draft.”

All three got to their feet again.

This time, the torches were on their right.

## On the Other Side of the Wall

By the time they returned to room three hundred, Pangpang had finished that piece of bread.

He found the spot where he'd been sitting, crouched down, and held a small piece of paper near the base of the wall.

The edge of the paper stirred.

Steve brought his torch closer. The flame bent, too.

“There's an open space on the other side,” Alex said.

They moved the stone pedestal aside. The wall behind it looked no different from the others, except for a narrow seam hidden along the bottom. Steve tapped along it a few times. It sounded different from the solid stone beside it.

Pangpang had already gone around to the other side.

There was a small stone button there, hidden behind the edge of the pedestal. You couldn't see it when you came in through the entrance, or when you sat beside the pedestal eating bread. It only came into view once the pedestal was moved.

He pressed it.

The wall slowly slid back in front of them.

There was no treasure chest.

No players waiting with weapons drawn.

Just a narrower passage.

Pangpang rested a hand on the doorframe and closed his eyes for a moment.

“And I was all ready to be amazed.”

Alex was the first to raise her torch. Air blew toward them from the passage, carrying a little dampness. Far away, they could just make out the sound of water, far fuller than the drip of water underground.

Like the sea.

Steve stood in the doorway and listened for a while. He checked his sword, then his inventory.

“How much bread have we got left?”

“Enough to keep us going for a bit,” Alex said.

“How far is a bit?” Pangpang asked.

No one said three hundred.

That made him feel a little better.

Before they left, Pangpang took a wooden sign from his inventory and nailed it beside the newly opened doorway. Alex thought he was going to write directions. Steve thought he was going to write “Watch out for traps.”

Pangpang wrote just two lines:

> We still haven't found the way out.
>
> I found this door.

He stepped back, felt something was missing, and added one more line:

> Leave it open for now.
