When he finished counting for the third time, Steve finally asked the question.

“Where did you get that box?”

Pangpang didn’t answer. He put the last stack of golden carrots back, closed the blue shulker box, then opened it again to make sure nothing had caught under the lid.

Three stacks of glistering melon slices. Two stacks of golden carrots.

The count hadn’t changed.

“You just counted them,” Alex said.

“The box was still open then.”

Steve looked at Alex.

Alex let that one pass.

## Five Stacks, One Box

These were the first valuable supplies they’d acquired through the village’s modded trades.

Pangpang had spotted the glistering melon slices first. Three stacks, their gold edges gleaming, displayed in the most prominent spot at the stall. They’d come in handy for brewing potions later, he said. Best to stock up now.

Steve and Alex didn’t think everything the three of them had saved up for should end up in his keeping.

Pangpang said he had somewhere to put it.

Then he pulled a blue shulker box out of his backpack.

Neither of them had seen him acquire it.

“Think of it as our storeroom,” Pangpang said.

They agreed, for the time being.

Pangpang promptly traded for two stacks of golden carrots and put those in the box as well. His regular backpack was cleared out, without so much as a scrap of carrot left behind.

Steve picked up the box and examined it.

“The storeroom’s portable?”

Pangpang put it in his inventory.

“It’s safer this way.”

“What happens when the storekeeper gets hungry?”

Pangpang pointed to the bread stall at the village entrance.

This time, Alex spoke up.

“We already spent our money on what’s in the storeroom.”

## He Was Always Quick to Grab the Gold

The next day, Steve and Alex came out of the mine with barely any coal to show for it. Pangpang, meanwhile, found a gold ingot by the roadside and headed straight for a villager’s stall.

When he returned, he gave Alex a crossbow and Steve an iron sword.

Steve tested the sword’s weight.

“You’re not a bad storekeeper.”

A few days later, they scraped together a stack of gold ingots in an abandoned mineshaft. When Pangpang carried off the gold again, neither of them stopped him.

Alex even started inspecting her crossbow, wondering whether she could trade up to an enchanted one.

Pangpang returned and slung an enchanted netherite sword across his own back.

It was very shiny.

The other two waited a moment.

No second sword appeared.

“Where’s the rest?” Steve asked.

Pangpang pointed to the sword. “It all went into this.”

Alex withdrew the hand she’d just held out.

“Next time you go shopping, tell us what you’re buying first.”

## Three Shares This Time

The next time Steve went mining, his haul was three stacks of iron ingots.

He’d barely set the iron down before Pangpang reached for it. Steve held down the iron. Alex held down Pangpang’s hand.

Pangpang stopped.

“Three shares this time.”

“Three shares of what?” Alex asked, keeping her grip.

“Diamond armor and a diamond pickaxe each. And a diamond sword for each of you. I don’t need one.”

Steve thought for a moment, then went to check the trade list at the stall. On this server, the villagers really did offer those trades.

He came back and nodded. Only then did they let go.

That day, all three of them got new gear. Later, once they’d gathered the upgrade materials, they all upgraded their chestplates to netherite. Steve chose diamond trim, Alex chose redstone, and Pangpang chose amethyst.

The three colors lined up in the sunlight.

After checking her chestplate, Alex suddenly said, “Open the box.”

Pangpang instinctively put a protective hand over his backpack.

“I’m not checking your personal stuff.” She pointed to the table. “I’m checking the storeroom.”

All five stacks were still there. Pangpang had looked after them meticulously. He’d even arranged the item icons in neat rows.

Steve found some paper and listed the supplies one by one. Beside him, Alex added: “Let everyone know before taking anything. Record the quantities afterward.”

Pangpang read it carefully.

“Then you two have to write it down when you take things, too.”

“Of course.”

“I still carry the box.”

“Fine.”

“You can’t take things while I’m away—”

Steve pointed to the paper. “That’s why we need a ledger.”

Pangpang finally closed the box.

As they were heading out, he wanted to open it for a fourth count. Alex handed him the ledger, and Steve pulled the door open.

“Where are we going today?” Pangpang asked.

“Mining for iron.”

“To buy what?”

Steve looked back at him.

“We haven’t decided. So we’re not buying anything today.”

Pangpang looked down and added a line at the bottom of the ledger.

> Storekeeper: Pangpang.

Alex took the pen and added three words.

> Subject to replacement.
