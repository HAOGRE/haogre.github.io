When the cleric handed Pangpang the ender pearl, all he said was, “It's taking up a bit of room.”

Pangpang looked at the pearl, then at his nearly empty backpack.

“I've got room.”

He tucked it deep inside, away from his two loaves of bread. A few steps later, he opened the backpack to check on it again. Steve reached over for a look. Pangpang swung the backpack to his other side.

“Looking won't make it disappear,” Steve said.

“Throwing will.”

Alex happened to be passing. “He hasn't said he wants to throw it.”

Pangpang nodded and fastened his backpack a little tighter.

## The Safest Slot

The three of them had only just moved into the village. They didn't own much worth showing off. Their room had three beds and a crafting table. The table still needed a decent leg.

The pearl was the first truly rare gift Pangpang had ever received.

He moved it from one end of his inventory to the other, tried three different slots, and finally settled on a corner where he wouldn't use it by accident.

Steve asked why he didn't put it in his hotbar.

“One slip and it's gone.”

“What if you need it in a hurry?”

Pangpang thought for a moment, then cleared a slot in his hotbar. The pearl stayed in his backpack.

“I'll put it there when I need it.”

Alex finished checking all three beds to make sure everyone's spawn point was set. They were heading to the mine for more iron. It wasn't far, and there was still daylight.

As they were leaving, the cleric asked how the pearl was working out.

Pangpang patted his backpack.

“Great. It's still there.”

Before the cleric could think of a reply, they were already out of the village.

## No Time to Think Twice

The grassy slope outside the mine was a little too green.

Steve stopped first.

Those green blocks weren't part of the ground. They were moving closer.

A creeper emerged from behind a tree, followed by a whole line of them. There were more at the top of the slope and more in the ditch. The server's mob event had spawned far too many. After a brief look, the three of them gave up counting.

“Go back!” Alex shouted.

The way back had turned green too.

Steve edged sideways with his shield raised. The first explosion went off close by. A chunk of ground collapsed, and more creepers moved into the gap. Two respawn notices quickly appeared in chat.

Pangpang backed up against a stone wall, his health already low.

He opened his backpack.

The pearl was still tucked away at the back.

This time, he didn't count his bread or rearrange any slots. He dragged the pearl into the space he'd left in his hotbar, looked up, picked a patch of level ground he could see clearly, and threw.

His view lurched.

The explosions were farther away now.

Pangpang landed on the opposite slope. The teleport took another bit of health. He hurried behind a rock and waited a while before he dared open his backpack.

The corner was empty.

He stared at it for a long time.

Then he ate a loaf of bread and started looking for the way back to the village.

## The Ones Who Got Home First

By the time Pangpang reached the village, he'd rehearsed the story three times in his head.

He would start with how many mobs there were. In the middle, he'd mention how well he'd aimed the pearl. Then he'd finish with how close he'd come to never making it home.

He pushed open the door.

Steve was fixing the table leg.

Alex sat beside him, checking the inventory that had been emptied when she'd respawned at her bed.

Pangpang stopped.

“How did you get here so fast?”

Steve glanced at the beds.

“We didn't walk.”

Pangpang told the whole story. When he got to the throw, Steve stopped with a plank halfway through the air.

“So you used it up?”

Pangpang nodded.

“But we can respawn.”

“I was standing next to three hundred creepers. I didn't have time to compare the cheapest ways home.”

Alex looked at his backpack.

“Still got your stuff?”

Pangpang laid his stone pickaxe, wood, and remaining bread on the table. Everything was there except the pearl.

Steve looked down at his own empty hands.

“Then we'll still have to go back for ours tomorrow.”

Pangpang immediately put his bread away.

“I can wait at the edge of the village.”

That evening, the three of them redrew the route for the next day. This time they would take the more open terrain and scout from a safe distance before going in.

Pangpang picked up the pen and drew a large circle around the grassy slope.

Steve thought he was going to write “Danger.”

Instead, Pangpang wrote:

> Pearl used here.

He thought for a moment, then added a smaller note underneath.

> Hurt a little to use it.
>
> But I'm not going back for it.
