Steve and Alex came back from hunting with just two blocks of cobblestone in their backpacks.

They’d set out with three.

“We used one to stand on,” Steve explained.

Pangpang pushed his bowl of instant noodles aside and looked at their empty hands.

“Where’s the meat?”

“It got away.”

“And you?”

“We came back.”

Pangpang stood up and flipped over the wooden sign by the door that read “Waiting for Meat.”

“Then the kitchen’s on its own today.”

## First, Something We Can Eat

This world had a cooking mod installed. There was now a pot beside the crafting table, and a recipe book beside the pot. Some slots in the book were lit up. Others still showed question marks.

Pangpang started with the ones that were lit up.

The next day, Steve was still sorting out his hunting gear when three plates of spaghetti appeared on the table. Long, thin strands curled around each plate, topped with red sauce.

Alex didn’t reach for a fork right away.

“Have you tried it this time?”

Pangpang pointed to the empty plate in the sink.

“Yes. My portion was bigger than yours.”

That reassured Steve.

When the three of them finished, only a little sauce remained. Pangpang put a marker on the recipe page and drew a check beside it with charcoal. Steve went to wash the plates. Alex tidied up the stove. Things could have ended there.

But Pangpang spotted the question mark on the next page.

Beside it was a picture of cocoa beans.

“Same pot,” he said. “Can’t be that different.”

Alex brought back one of the freshly washed plates.

“Start with a small portion.”

## A Unanimous Verdict

Pangpang did not make a small portion.

When he brought out the new dish, Steve thought the bottom of the pot had fallen onto the plate.

A dark brown mass sat in the middle. It was piled high, with an uneven surface. It leaned a little to the left, then slowly settled, like a block of dirt deciding whether to collapse.

“What’s it called?” Alex asked.

Pangpang glanced at the recipe book. The question mark was still there.

“No name yet.”

Steve nodded. “No rush.”

They each took a little. Steve stopped first, then Alex, and finally Pangpang. The only sound in the room came from the stove.

Pangpang put down his spoon.

“Maybe it’s gone cold.”

Alex pushed the plate back toward the stove. Once it was warmed through, they got three clean spoons and each took another taste.

This time, all three stopped at once.

“Now we know for sure,” Steve said.

“Know what?”

“It tastes bad at any temperature.”

Pangpang didn’t argue. He was the cook, and he’d just eaten it twice himself.

They didn’t serve the leftovers to the villagers. They didn’t give the dish a nicer name and keep putting it on the table, either. Alex fetched the scrap bucket, and Steve started scrubbing the pot.

Pangpang took on the job of erasing the check beside the recipe.

“That check was for the pasta,” Alex reminded him.

Pangpang drew it back in, then put an X on the cocoa bean page.

The X was bigger than the check.

## The Question Mark Becomes a Cup

That afternoon, Steve and Alex went to repair the fence. Pangpang stayed in the kitchen, staring at the remaining cocoa beans.

This time, he really did use only a small amount.

The mod’s cooking station let him put ingredients into different slots. Pangpang changed the container and tried the recipe again. The brown mass didn’t reappear in the pot. Instead, a cup of dark liquid appeared in the output slot.

The floating item label read: Coffee.

Pangpang stared at the name for a while, then looked back at the ingredient slots.

“This mod of yours,” he told the crafting table, “is pretty casual about names.”

He took a sip.

He didn’t put the cup down right away.

A small icon appeared on his status bar. He looked up. The cupboard he’d left half organized, unable to face finishing it, suddenly looked like just the thing to organize right now.

By the time Steve returned, the bowls were arranged by size, the recipes were sorted, and Pangpang had even given the stubbornly dirty pot another scrub.

“Did you finish eating the bad stuff?” Steve asked.

Pangpang handed him the new cup. “Something else. Just try a little.”

After drinking, Alex opened the status display to check. The game called the effect “Energized,” and its timer was counting down.

“This one can stay,” she said.

Pangpang finally made the X on the cocoa page a little smaller.

He didn’t erase it. The failed recipe got a line of its own, so he wouldn’t forget and repeat the entire failure later.

That evening, a new sign appeared by the door.

It had three lines: Spaghetti, available; Coffee, limited quantities; New dishes, on hold.

Steve read it and tapped the last line.

“On hold until when?”

Pangpang poked his head out of the kitchen.

“Until you’re willing to try again.”

Alex added four words at the end of that line.

> Date to be determined.
