The butcher gave them three buns stuffed with rabbit and a bucket of cod.

The buns were easy to divide. One each.

The fish was trickier.

Pangpang held the bucket. Steve watched the fish. Alex asked whether the three of them planned to take turns looking after it.

The fish turned in the bucket.

“Let's put it in the river,” Pangpang said. “We can still keep the bucket.”

They walked down to the river, tipped the cod into the water, then filled the bucket with water to take home. All three thought that was the end of it.

The next day, the fish came back with a diamond sword.

## A Fish's Inventory

The sword floated up first, followed by a bow and three stacks of arrows, then a golden axe and a golden carrot.

The cod poked its head up beside them.

Steve looked at the sword, then at the fish.

“Where did it get all this?”

Alex checked their surroundings first, making sure no player was tossing items from a distance. Pangpang crouched down and set the empty bucket behind him.

“Maybe this server has a river quest.”

Their server had plenty of custom events. Villagers could hand out buns; chests could fall from the sky. A fish delivering gifts wasn't the strangest thing they'd seen.

Steve got the sword. Alex put away the bow and arrows. Pangpang held the golden axe and tucked the golden carrot into his inventory.

He would have liked a closer look at that fish's inventory.

The fish had already sunk out of sight.

Back home, Steve laid the diamond sword on the table and looked it over twice. Alex reminded him to shut the door. Pangpang found a wooden sign, planning to mark the spot by the river.

He had meant to write “Release fish, get rewards.”

Alex saw it and took the pen.

“Just write what happened today.”

So the sign became: We received some items here today.

Steve thought it was too long. Pangpang thought it didn't sound impressive enough.

By the next day, they all thought it still wasn't cautious enough.

## Yesterday's Gifts, Taken Back Today

Ripples appeared on the river.

This wasn't yesterday's little cod.

A character from one of the mods rose from the river, draped in shimmering water. Without a word, it looked over their equipment.

Their inventories flashed a few times.

The diamond sword was gone. The bow and arrows were gone. So was the golden axe.

Pangpang checked his inventory. Yesterday's golden carrot had vanished too, leaving only the thirty-two he had collected since.

Steve reached down and felt his empty scabbard.

“Gifts come with a return policy?”

“We didn't see any terms yesterday,” Alex said.

“Still haven't.”

The figure sank back into the river.

Pangpang stood on the bank without moving for a long time. Steve thought he was angry and was about to suggest they leave when Pangpang took the thirty-two golden carrots out of his inventory.

“Think it through before you throw those in,” Alex said at once.

Pangpang did think for a while.

Then he threw them all in.

The water stayed quiet for a few seconds.

Steve was about to fetch yesterday's sign when items began popping onto the bank, one after another: forty-four golden carrots and a full stack of glistering melon slices.

Pangpang crouched down and counted them three times.

“Forty-four,” he said.

“I heard you,” Steve said.

“And sixty-four slices.”

“Heard that too.”

Alex stopped him from throwing anything else into the water.

“Do you know the rules now?”

Pangpang looked at the river.

Yesterday, they'd released a fish and received weapons. Today, the weapons had been taken back. He'd thrown in carrots and got more supplies in return. Every one of those things had happened. None was guaranteed to happen again.

“No,” he said.

“Then let's head back for now.”

## The Bucket Was Still There

On the way home, Steve kept asking the same question.

“How come you ended up with more stuff?”

Pangpang had no answer. He stored the stack of glistering melon slices separately. They were brewing ingredients. Shiny as they were, you couldn't just eat them like ordinary melon slices.

Alex sat down by the door and recorded the past two days' inventory changes, one by one.

Steve went to find a new iron sword.

Pangpang picked up the empty bucket to fetch water from the well. As he passed the river, he could see yesterday's wooden sign still standing in the distance.

“We received some items here today.”

He turned the sign over and thought for a long time, but couldn't come up with a sentence that would always be true.

In the end, he wrote a single word.

> Riverbank.

This time, he was pleased with it.

A little later, the cod poked its head above the water again.

Pangpang lifted the bucket a little higher.

“I'm just passing through today.”
