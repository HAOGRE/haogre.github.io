When the five of them entered the third level, Pangpang held his shield the highest.

The first two levels had already taught him a habit: shield up first, check the rewards later.

This time, nothing lunged at them.

Supplies lay on the floor, and clusters of revival spirits glowed softly in the corners. They were items from this mod’s dungeon: stash them in your pack, and each could help you out once when it mattered.

The sign read:

> Ten levels in this dungeon.
>
> Level Three: Reward Room.

Pangpang slowly lowered his shield.

“Can we get more levels like this?”

Steve was already filling his pack.

## Full Packs, No Way Out

Alex packed away the supplies and made a circuit of the room, following the walls.

Then she went around again.

“Where’s the exit?”

Pangpang had just filled his last inventory slot. He looked up.

The entrance was still behind them, but the way to level four was nowhere to be found.

The Deep Valley Miner had tapped on the walls. They sounded solid. The Ender Hunter checked the ceiling. No levers. Steve circled the sign twice and discovered only that the back was blank too.

“It says ten levels. Surely they can’t call it a day at three,” he said.

All five started digging.

A row of shallow holes appeared in the left wall, then another in the right. Pangpang worked along the bottom. With nowhere to put the stone he dug out, he had to pile it back up around his feet.

When Alex looked over, he had nearly walled himself in.

“Why are you building a house?”

“I’m not,” Pangpang said. “My pack’s full.”

They tried every surface they could hit.

No door.

The revival spirits glowed inside their packs. They hadn’t used a single one.

“They gave us all these revives,” Pangpang said, leaning on his pickaxe. “Are they expecting us to die looking for the exit?”

Nobody answered.

Everyone was staring at the walls.

## Enough Digging

Pangpang stepped out of his pile of stone and walked over to the sign.

It stood on a broad wooden base. He gave it a look. Had to be more comfortable than sitting on rubble.

“Taking a break.”

He sat down.

Click.

Four heads turned at once.

Pangpang looked down too.

The wooden base sank a little. The sign swung aside, and the stone wall behind it slowly slid back along a neat seam.

A doorway appeared.

Steve stood with his pickaxe raised, still poised to take another swing at the wall.

Alex slowly pushed his hand down.

“You knew there was a mechanism underneath?” the Ender Hunter asked.

Pangpang shook his head.

“Then why did you sit here?”

“Tired.”

The Deep Valley Miner looked at the rubble on the floor, then at his pickaxe.

“Hard to learn from an answer like that.”

Pangpang started to get up.

“Don’t move yet,” all four said at once.

They filed through the doorway. Steve stayed behind until last, watching as Pangpang slowly stood up. Only when he was sure the door wasn’t closing did he let out a breath.

“Let’s go.”

Pangpang brushed off his trousers. “Next time we’re looking for a hidden mechanism, let me take a break first.”

## The Third Spot

There were three boss markers on level four.

The first boss was a lizard warrior consumed by resentment. It charged with its blade raised, looking fierce, but went down quickly.

The second was a block golem consumed by resentment. It had barely raised its fist when its core was exposed. Steve and Alex attacked from the front and back, never letting that punch land.

The five of them looked toward the third spot.

There was nothing there.

Pangpang carefully circled it with his shield up.

No footprints from an invisible enemy. No health bar suddenly appearing. Not even a moving rock.

The Ender Hunter waited a while, then lowered his bow.

“Where’s the third one?”

Pangpang pointed at the empty spot.

“Maybe it’s tired too.”

Steve looked at him.

Pangpang immediately took a step to the side.

“Fine. I won’t sit down this time.”
