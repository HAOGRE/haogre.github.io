When the villager held up six fingers, Pangpang thought the price was a little steep.

Steve looked at the six carrots in Pangpang’s hand.

“It’s a house.”

Pangpang took another look at the house.

It had walls, a roof, and a door. But once you stepped through the door, you were nearly at the opposite wall.

“Then it has to come with a bed,” Pangpang said.

The villager led them inside and pointed to several beds stacked against the wall.

All three agreed on the spot.

## Decorating Starts with Moving the Beds

This was their first house in the new world.

After spawning in, the three had run a few errands for the villagers, but they hadn’t saved up much. A house for six carrots was a special deal the village offered new neighbors. Once they’d paid, they had to figure out dinner all over again.

Still, they finally had somewhere to sleep.

Steve put his bed in first. Alex measured out the space and placed the second. When Pangpang carried the third bed through the door, he realized he was standing in the only spot left for it.

“I’ll step outside,” he said.

Once the beds were in place, the three stood outside and looked in.

Very tidy.

Very full.

Alex pointed to the middle. “We need room to walk.”

Steve shifted his bed to the left, and Pangpang moved his to the right. After a couple of rounds of shuffling, they finally got the door to open all the way.

The crafting table went under the window. The furnace was tucked into the wall. The chest couldn’t block the door or cover a bed.

Pangpang suggested putting the chest outside.

Steve pointed to a player wandering nearby.

Pangpang carried the chest back in.

In the end, they kept their most important belongings with them and used the chest at home for everyday materials. Pangpang set aside a slot for food. By that evening, it was empty.

## Nothing but the Floor

In time, all three had iron armor and weapons. Pangpang had also saved up a supply of golden carrots and glistering melon slices that he couldn’t bring himself to use. The house stayed the same size. It just kept acquiring more shelves.

One day, they came home to find the door gone.

Then they noticed the walls were gone too.

A passing player had torn down the house and left. The wood particles hadn’t even disappeared yet. The beds sat under the open sky, the crafting table lay nearby, and where a painting had once hung, there was now a clear view of the mountains.

Steve stood on the doorstep.

It was the most house-like thing left.

“What about our gear?” Alex asked.

All three checked their inventories at once.

Everything was still there.

Pangpang checked his golden carrots again and tucked them a little farther away.

“Then we move,” he said.

“Where to?”

He pointed at the ground.

“Down there.”

## Twelve Blocks Down

This time, they didn’t dig straight down.

Steve dug a staircase beside the old house. Alex placed torches along the way, and Pangpang hauled the cobblestone up to the surface. Twelve blocks down, they stopped and dug sideways to make a stone room.

Exit first. Furniture second.

Alex said it twice.

Pangpang had already come down carrying a bed.

Underground, they weren’t limited by the walls of the old house. They spaced out the three beds and left an aisle two blocks wide down the middle. The furnace and crafting table went on the other side. The chests stood against the wall, with nothing above them.

Steve tried walking from the doorway to the far end, then turned around and walked back.

He didn’t bump into Pangpang’s bed.

“We’re getting better at this,” he said.

The villagers came by with bread—a stack apiece.

When the first villager came downstairs, Pangpang was looking for the old wooden sign. When the second came down, Steve added a door to the entrance. By the third, Alex was beginning to suspect they’d made the stairs a little too narrow.

Pangpang took the bread and was about to put it in a chest. Then he decided he should eat some first.

“Still saving the golden carrots?” Steve asked.

“Still saving them.”

“And the glistering melon slices?”

“For brewing.”

“What about the bread?”

Pangpang had already taken a bite.

“This I can use right now.”

## A Roof You Couldn’t See

That evening, the three stood on the surface to inspect the entrance.

From a distance, it looked like an ordinary patch of grass. Only a little of the foundation remained of the house they’d bought for six carrots. Their new home was hidden beneath it, with torchlight spilling out of the stairwell.

Alex reminded them that hidden didn’t mean impossible to find. They split their important supplies between different spots and still took their usual gear whenever they went out.

Pangpang retrieved the wooden sign from the old house and nailed it up beside the entrance.

“Doesn’t that tell everyone where we live?” Steve asked.

Pangpang thought about it, pulled the sign down, and took it downstairs.

He hung it on the wall opposite the three beds.

The old lettering had been rubbed away. The new letters were small.

> House price: six carrots.
>
> Basement: dug it ourselves.

Alex read it and pointed to the wide aisle down the middle.

“Put that on there too.”

Pangpang added one more line.

> The door opens all the way now.
