When the egg landed on Pangpang’s head, he was watching Steve feed the cow.

Thonk.

Pangpang clutched his head, then looked up.

An ender dragon flew overhead as if nothing had happened.

Alex picked the egg up off the ground. The shell cracked, and a baby ender dragon crawled out and sneezed at her.

A little blue flame flickered.

Pangpang looked at it, then rubbed his head again.

“I got it first.”

The little dragon crawled over to Alex’s feet.

## Following Someone Doesn’t Make Them Your Owner

The pet mod in this world had already given them one problem to solve.

Steve had a cow that looked small but weighed 231 tonnes. They had laid a stone path for it, repaired the floor, and even put up a “No Cows Upstairs” sign at the foot of the stairs.

The cow was gentle.

Being gentle didn’t stop it from crushing floorboards.

Now a little dragon that breathed blue fire had joined the camp. Pangpang circled it twice, but it kept following Alex.

When Alex went left, it went left. When she stopped, it sat down and looked up at her.

“I think it’s made its choice,” Steve said.

Alex glanced at the bowstring she was adjusting, then at the little blue sparks escaping from the dragon’s mouth.

“I haven’t.”

She opened the mod’s pet menu, set the dragon to follow Pangpang, and carried it over to him.

Pangpang held out his hands.

The dragon sniffed him, climbed onto his shoulder, and breathed a small puff of fire beside his ear.

Pangpang immediately put it back on the ground.

“You can be mine. Just stay off my shoulder for now.”

## Alex Gets a Pet Too

That afternoon, a wither flew in from the distance.

Steve grabbed his shield. Pangpang backed toward the house. Alex had just raised her bow when the wither suddenly shrank and floated down beside her, its three heads turning toward them one after another.

No explosion.

No attack, either.

A new name appeared in the pet menu.

It circled Alex once, then hovered beside her shoulder. The left head watched her bow, the right watched the door, and the middle one stared at Pangpang.

Pangpang pointed at it. “You want this one?”

She watched it for a while, checked that its follow mode was working properly, and nodded.

Pangpang looked down at his dragon. It opened its mouth and spat blue sparks at a bare stone.

He made no further suggestion of a swap.

Steve stepped away from the wall and began taking stock.

“One cow, one dragon, one wither.”

“Three people,” Pangpang added.

Alex looked at the three little heads.

“Don’t count heads.”

## Getting Out the Door Is a Job in Itself

The next day, they planned to go hunting.

Steve had picked up his sword before he noticed that the cow had followed him to the door. He lured it back onto the stone floor with some wheat, shut the fence gate, and turned to get his backpack.

The little dragon slipped out after Pangpang.

Pangpang took it back inside. He had barely closed the door when the little wither floated out over it.

Alex glanced at the sky and opened the pet menu again.

The three of them stood in the yard, setting each pet to stay put.

Steve handled the cow. Pangpang handled the dragon. Alex handled the wither.

The cow needed solid ground, with no storage room underneath. There couldn’t be any wooden crates beside the dragon; its blue fire would trigger the mod’s burning effect if it hit them. The little wither didn’t wreck the house, but it did like hovering over anyone who came through the door, all three heads watching them at once.

Pangpang had planned to leave some bread in the yard.

The dragon sniffed it and showed no interest.

“What does it eat?” Steve asked.

Pangpang checked the feeding section of the pet menu, put the bread away, and went back for the right feed.

By the time all three of them finally stood outside the camp gate, the sun was already a little higher than they’d expected.

Steve looked back to check.

The cow hadn’t followed.

The dragon hadn’t followed.

Neither had the wither.

“Let’s go,” he said.

Pangpang took two steps, then stopped.

“I think I forgot something.”

Alex waited for him to remember. Steve looked into the yard. All three pets were there.

Pangpang held out his empty hands.

“My sword.”

He ran back to the house to get it.

As soon as the door opened, all three of the little wither’s heads turned toward him. The dragon crawled to the edge of the stone platform. The cow stayed where it was and mooed at him.

Pangpang stood with his sword in hand, looking at the three pets.

“I’m not back,” he said.

“I haven’t left yet.”
