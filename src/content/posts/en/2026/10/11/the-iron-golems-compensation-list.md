Pangpang’s gold hat used to be a shield.

He’d found the shield in a chest that fell from the sky. It gleamed when he picked it up and looked impressive when he held it aloft. It also cracked in no time.

Pangpang couldn’t bear to throw it away. He saved the pieces and took them home, where he pieced them together into a crooked hat. It offered no protection, but when he stood in the sun, you could spot him from a long way off.

“Makes you easy to spot,” Steve said.

“There aren’t any enemies in the village,” Pangpang said.

That day, the village’s iron golem hadn’t quite worked out who its enemies were.

## Who Gets the House

A guard quest mod was active in this save’s village, and it sometimes got friend and foe mixed up. The three of them had barely entered the village when the iron golem raised its arms.

A moment later, Steve opened his eyes beside his bed.

A moment after that, Alex was back too.

They sat at the respawn point for a while, then decided to try another way into the village.

Another way didn’t help.

The iron golem was tall enough to see them over the wall.

On their third attempt, Pangpang brought up the rear. Steve was just about to explain when the iron golem suddenly turned toward Pangpang.

Steve and Alex instinctively ducked aside.

The iron golem didn’t hit him. It just looked down at the gold hat.

Pangpang slowly took it off and held it out.

The iron golem took the hat, then placed three ender pearls and a wooden sign in his hands.

The sign had a picture of a nearby house on it.

The blacksmith hurried over, glanced at the sign, and said they could live in the house.

Steve straightened up.

“We’re the ones who got hit.”

The blacksmith nodded.

“He’s the one getting the stuff.”

The blacksmith nodded again.

Pangpang was already trying the door.

“No need to share a key,” he called back. “The door just opens.”

## From Three to a Full Stack

The next day, the iron golem came back.

Steve immediately hid behind the table. Alex pushed open a window to check whether escaping that way would land her in the vegetable patch. Pangpang stood in the doorway, wondering whether he should find another hat.

This time, the iron golem had only brought pearls.

Together with yesterday’s three, they made exactly sixteen.

The blacksmith followed behind, translating. “It’s apologizing for hurting you by mistake earlier.”

Steve peered out from behind the table.

“Could it look at who’s getting paid and try apologizing again?”

Pangpang held out two pearls, one for each of them.

But the blacksmith handed the pearls back to Pangpang, then fetched two iron chestplates and gave them to Steve and Alex.

“You need these more.”

Alex put on her chestplate and rolled her shoulders to test the fit.

“Now that’s sound judgment.”

Steve found a sheet of paper and carefully wrote: Two people injured by mistake: two chestplates. One gold hat taken: one house. Sixteen pearls: temporarily held by Pangpang.

Pangpang leaned over to look.

“Why ‘temporarily’?”

“Because you just erased the word ‘shared.’”

## The Bell Doesn’t Need to Be This Close

That evening, Steve and Alex had just stepped outside when they heard something overhead.

They looked up.

A huge copper bell dropped over them, trapping both inside.

The yard went quiet.

Pangpang was sitting indoors eating bread. After a while, he heard two knocks from outside.

“Who is it?” he asked.

Steve’s voice came from inside the bell, with a slight echo.

“Your other two payees.”

Pangpang put down his bread and went out with a pickaxe. The three of them worked from inside and outside for a long time before they managed to dig an opening through the bell.

Alex crawled out first. Steve followed, catching the shoulder of his chestplate on the edge and making it ring again.

The blacksmith stood nearby. He had brought the remaining pieces of iron armor to complete both their sets.

Steve took the boots and said nothing for a long while.

“Still keeping that compensation list?” Pangpang asked.

“Yes.”

“Who’s paying this time?”

Steve looked up at the empty sky.

“Let’s just write down what happened first.”

After dark, the three of them moved the table against a wall with no windows. The house was sturdy, the door closed properly, and so far, nothing was happening on the roof.

Pangpang picked up the increasingly crowded sheet of paper, wanting to count his sixteen pearls one more time.

Steve held down one corner of the paper.

“We’re not going outside tomorrow.”

“Then what are you going to do?”

Alex leaned her pickaxe beside the bed.

“Wait for the rest of the deliveries to land.”
