Steve nailed the last plank across the gate and took two steps back.

“Now we're safe.”

Pangpang tugged on the gate.

It didn't open.

“True. We can't get out either.”

## They Beat a Bull, Then Feared the Neighbors

The three of them had just started a new modded world. Alex hadn't even collected a full stack of wood when they ran into a one-ton bull.

They couldn't beat it in a fight.

So they dug a pit.

Steve dug, Alex covered the opening, and Pangpang's job was to lure the bull over, then get out of the way in time.

When the bull charged into the trap, all three of them were still using starter tools.

Once they'd collected the drops, Pangpang looked at the pit, pleased.

“Next time there's trouble, we'll just let it fall in.”

They took what they'd gathered into the village and bought a house with a yard. As they moved in, an iron golem came walking toward them from the far end of the village road.

Pangpang stopped first.

Steve stopped too.

Alex looked at those two iron arms, then at their newly purchased wooden gate.

None of them wanted to find out firsthand which was harder to deal with: a bull or an iron golem.

The iron golem was only on patrol. But the more they talked, the less reassured they felt. In the end, they agreed: fewer trips outside, less trading.

Within days, the walls were higher, the bar across the gate was thicker, and a trench filled with thorns ran around the outside.

Being self-sufficient kept them busy. Farming, cooking, sorting their storage—they walked back and forth across the yard plenty of times each day.

They just never walked out through the gate.

## Before the First Knock

A player looking for trouble stood outside the wall and shouted a couple of times.

Nobody opened the gate.

He raised his weapon, ready to step forward and give it a whack.

Before he could, the ground gave way beneath his feet.

There was a noise from the thorn trench, followed by the blast of a modded trap. The player vanished, leaving loot scattered on the ground.

Inside, Pangpang put down his bowl.

“Did he knock?”

“He didn't get that far,” said Alex.

The three of them gathered up the items by the trench and laid the equipment out on the table. A full set of iron armor, an iron sword, an iron pickaxe, a shield, and a bow for each of them. Then they counted out sixty-four arrows apiece.

One set. Another. One more.

Steve put the spare items in a chest.

“Didn't have to lift a finger.”

Pangpang tried out his shield. It felt sturdy.

“So, shall we go out now?”

They heard the iron golem's footsteps outside the yard. All three paused.

“Tomorrow.”

## This Time, from Above

The next morning, Pangpang woke to an enormous crash.

There was a large crate beside his bed.

And a hole in the roof.

Still lying there, he looked at the crate, then up at the roof.

Steve and Alex rushed in, having just thrown on their gear.

“Who got in?”

Pangpang pointed up.

The gate had held. The roof was another matter.

None of them had seen where the crate had come flying from. When they opened it, there were no weapons and no enemies.

Just cakes.

Pangpang took one out. More underneath. He took out another. More behind it.

This time, he didn't rush off to find a spoon.

“How long is it going to take the three of us to eat all this?”

Alex looked out the window. The villagers were setting up tables in the square.

“Let's take it out and share it.”

Steve looked at the gate.

Then at the crate.

Finally, he picked up a tool to pry off the planks.

## First, Clear a Path

They put their weapons back in the house, removed the planks blocking the gate, moved the stone blocks aside, and built a safe wooden bridge across the trench.

Clearing the gateway took longer than dealing with the troublemaker had.

Steve and Alex carried the crate between them. Pangpang led the way, holding the first slice of cake.

As they approached the iron golem, all three still slowed their steps.

The iron golem looked at them, then continued its patrol.

No punches.

Pangpang looked down at his cake. The frosting was still intact.

A crowd soon gathered in the square. The cakes came out of the crate one by one. When they ran out of table space, the villagers brought over another table.

By evening, the three of them were carrying the empty crate home.

Out of habit, Steve reached for a plank.

Alex laid a hand on it.

“We still have to return the tables tomorrow.”

Pangpang was already in the yard, looking up at the roof.

“That, we can patch up.”
