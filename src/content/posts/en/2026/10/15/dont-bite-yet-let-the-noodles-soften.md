Pangpang put a dry noodle cake on the table.

Steve picked it up and inspected both sides.

“New shield?”

“Food.”

Steve opened his mouth.

“Don't bite yet.”

## This World Starts Empty

This was a different, brand-new world.

No old house. No chests full of food. When the three of them spawned, they even had to collect their wood from scratch.

They found a village and borrowed a place to stay. They soon realized that supplies here were tight.

There wasn't much flour left, and the kitchen was missing all sorts of equipment. Before Pangpang could try a new dish, he had to lay out what was available.

A basin.

A board.

A borrowed pot.

Steve surveyed the arrangement. “What's missing?”

Pangpang looked at him.

“Someone willing to knead.”

## The First Kind Still Needs Boiling

The world had a cooking mod. The recipe page listed both spaghetti and instant noodles.

Neither looked particularly complicated.

Only when the dough was on the board did Steve discover that the recipe page hadn't mentioned aching arms.

He held one end while Pangpang rolled the other. Alex took the sheets and tried to cut even strands.

The first batch wasn't very even.

Some strands looked like rope. Others looked like shoelaces.

Pangpang boiled only a small portion. Steve stood beside the pot, expecting it all to become one lump.

But when the noodles came out, they still separated.

With a little sauce made from what they could gather, they looked like a proper meal.

The villager who had come to retrieve the pot tried a bite in the doorway and forgot to take the pot away.

“Just from those things you had?”

Steve held out his two aching arms.

“And a little of these.”

Pangpang set the finished long noodles apart from the next test portion.

“That's spaghetti. It still needs boiling before you eat it. For the other kind, I want to try something that softens with hot water poured over it.”

## First, a Small Failure

Steve watched carefully when the first test cake went into water.

Its edges softened.

Its center didn't move.

By the time they could loosen the middle, the outside had turned to mush.

Alex lifted it for a look.

“They won't separate now.”

Pangpang noted the problem beside the recipe: too thick, too tightly packed.

They didn't pour in the rest of the flour. Supplies were scarce; they couldn't afford to lose whole pots of food to trial and error. Each adjustment used only a small portion.

Pangpang checked the mod's cooking and drying steps again. Alex arranged the strands more loosely. Steve watched the fire.

Eventually, they had the dry noodle cake from the beginning of this story.

It was light.

And hard.

Which was why Steve had asked whether it was a shield.

## Wait This Time

Pangpang placed the cake in a bowl, poured in hot water, and covered it.

Steve reached for it. Alex stopped him.

“You were just complaining it was hard.”

“I'm checking if it's changed.”

“You just put it down.”

The villagers who had come to watch stayed too. Everyone gathered around a covered bowl as if it held a dragon egg about to hatch.

When Pangpang finally lifted the lid, the noodles had loosened.

Steve nudged them with his chopsticks.

This time, he could lift strands from both the outside and the middle.

A villager tried a bite, then looked at the hearth, where the fire had already gone out.

“You didn't boil the noodles again?”

“They were processed earlier,” Pangpang said. “This step only needs hot water.”

Steve picked up the other bowl, the spaghetti, and compared them.

The same little supply of flour now offered two ways to eat.

The villagers discussed which kind suited a meal at home and which could be packed in small portions for a trip.

Pangpang tied the flour sack shut. A new recipe hadn't made the sack fuller. They still had to count out the next meal's share.

They hadn't tested how long the noodle cakes would keep, either. For now, they made only enough to eat soon.

Steve lifted some noodles from his bowl and finally found time to speak.

“I thought you'd just made the noodles hard.”

Alex handed him the lid.

“Next time, remember to wait until they're soft.”
