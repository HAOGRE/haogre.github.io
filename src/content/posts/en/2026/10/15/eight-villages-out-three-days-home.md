When Steve stopped, the first thing he checked was that there were no footsteps behind him.

Then he checked that there was no village anywhere around him.

Finally, he began to think the second discovery was not so good.

## The Third Time Wasn't an Accident

It started with a strange item from the mine.

Their modded world included a manure block nobody particularly wanted to collect.

The first time Steve dug one up, he tossed it aside. It landed on Alex's head.

He said he hadn't aimed properly.

The second time, he carried it home and put it on Alex's bed.

Even Pangpang couldn't find an excuse for that one.

The sheets were washed. Compensation was paid. Steve seemed to behave himself for a while.

Until the next mining trip.

He brought back a large bundle and, when Alex wasn't looking, dumped it over her, half burying her.

Alex pushed the stuff aside and stood up.

Steve ran before she said a word.

## Eight Villages Went By

At the first village, he was still laughing.

At the second, he glanced back.

Alex was still there.

By the third, he was concentrating on running.

Past wheat fields, around a bell tower, through the gap between two houses. Steve chose whatever route he could run through and never noted which way he'd entered.

Alex was keeping track of his back.

By the eighth village, neither of them was talking.

Steve plunged into the woods, circled several thick trees, and finally left the footsteps behind.

He leaned against a trunk, panting.

“At last—”

He didn't finish.

Beyond the trees were more trees.

## Nobody Had Watched the Road

Steve walked in one direction and, a while later, came upon the same rock.

He changed direction.

For now, no rock. Still no village.

Elsewhere, Alex discovered a similar problem. The nearly identical forks they'd passed looked just as nearly identical going back.

Neither of them had brought a record of the route home.

On the first day, they searched separately.

After dark, each had to find shelter from rain and mobs.

On the second day, Steve started rationing the food in his pack. At high points he stopped to search for a familiar bell tower; in the valleys he worried that he'd lost the direction again.

Alex followed a stream toward the edge of the woods, watching for another set of erratic footprints.

On the third day, they finally found each other near the forest's edge.

Steve raised his hands first.

“I was wrong.”

Alex looked at him without speaking.

“I'll wash the sheets again.”

Still nothing.

“And clean the yard.”

Alex shared a little of her remaining food with him.

“Find the way first.”

## Together This Time

They were no longer running and chasing.

Steve remembered the bridges they'd crossed. Alex recognized the slopes along the way. At each fork, both stopped to check. If they took a wrong turn, they retraced their steps.

At last, a familiar bell tower appeared beyond a hill.

Steve stared at it for a long time.

“That one's ours, right?”

“Don't run toward it.”

Three days and three nights had passed. When they reached the village gate, Pangpang was waiting there.

Pangpang looked at their mud-covered boots.

“How many dungeons did you explore?”

“No dungeons,” Steve said.

“What did you bring back, then?”

Alex set her walking stick beside the gate.

“This.”

Steve set down another.

Pangpang served them hot food, then asked how many villages they'd passed.

“Eight.”

“And you didn't stop at any of them?”

Both lowered their heads and ate.

After the meal, Steve steadied himself against the table, stood up, and picked up a bucket.

Alex glanced at him.

“Where are you going?”

“To wash the sheets.”

This time, he walked slowly. And he stayed inside the yard.
