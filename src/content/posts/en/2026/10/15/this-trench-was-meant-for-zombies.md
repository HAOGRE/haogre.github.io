Pangpang was the first to spot the blue gleam on the mountain road.

“Diamond ore?”

Alex watched for a moment.

“It moves.”

More than two hundred players in diamond armor were heading for the village.

## Equipped, but Not Friendly

Their leader stepped forward and told the villagers to hand over their valuables.

Steve held the fence and glanced back at the storeroom.

Then he looked at his own iron armor.

This did not look like a crowd they could talk into leaving.

But there was also a trench outside the village.

When they had first dug it, Pangpang had asked Steve how many zombies he expected.

Steve had said a little extra room couldn't hurt.

So everyone dug along the village boundary, turned the corner, and kept digging. They left a separate safe approach to the gate.

Usually, the trench was easy to overlook.

The player at the front overlooked it today, too.

## Those Behind Didn't Stop

The first sound was diamond boots scraping an earthen wall.

The second was someone colliding with the person in front.

After the third, counting became difficult.

Those at the front tried to back up. Those behind kept coming. Some dodged sideways and found more trench; others had barely turned around before a companion knocked them in.

Diamond armor clattered against diamond armor.

Steve stood behind the fence. He hadn't even reached for his sword.

Pangpang opened his mouth, then closed it.

By the time the mountain road went quiet, the entire crowd was trapped in the ditch.

“This trench,” Pangpang said slowly, “was meant for zombies.”

Alex peered down.

“They didn't ask.”

## The Guard Gets to Work

This wasn't an ordinary survival server. The village guard mod gave the iron golem extra damage, and raiders had custom loot rules.

All that gleaming diamond armor therefore bought the trapped crowd very little time.

The iron golem arrived and worked its way along the trench.

After a burst of clanging, the raiders were sent back to their spawn points.

What they left behind was even harder to count than the players had been.

Helmets, chestplates, tools, and extra diamond drops.

Pangpang stared into the trench for a long time.

“How many empty chests do we have?”

## Put the Ladders in First

The villagers brought ladders and crates, then climbed down from the safe side to collect the loot.

One crate reached the square, then another followed. Steve sorted the items, Alex checked the equipment, and Pangpang crouched nearby looking for suitable leggings.

He found a pair, then spotted another.

Holding up both, he asked, “What about these and these?”

“They're identical.”

“Then what have I been comparing?”

By the end of the distribution, every villager had a sizable pile of diamonds.

Steve, Alex, and Pangpang finally had complete sets of diamond armor, too.

Pangpang raised his arms and took a few steps, listening to the new equipment clink.

“This morning, we were still deciding which mine to visit.”

Steve looked toward the village entrance.

The iron golem had resumed its patrol. With the loot removed, the trench was once again just a ditch in the dirt.

Alex locked the remaining diamonds in the storeroom and took out a shovel.

“They damaged the sides. We should patch them up.”

Pangpang looked down at his brand-new diamond armor.

“We've just upgraded, and we're digging dirt?”

Steve took the other shovel.

“That's where we found the upgrade.”
