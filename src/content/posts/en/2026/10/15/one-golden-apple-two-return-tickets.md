The mayor's voice was especially gentle as he handed Pangpang the golden apple.

“Sit down and eat.”

Pangpang accepted it, looked at the apple, then toward the village gate.

Steve and Alex had just been sent away.

## At Least the House Was Cheap

In this new world, with its village interaction mod, the three friends had only wanted somewhere to live.

Steve had taken them the long way around.

He'd played another prank on Alex with one of the mod's manure blocks, and she had chased him. Across a large stretch of the map they ran, until they stumbled into a village.

By the time Pangpang caught up, Steve was already talking to a villager about a house.

There was an empty one near the entrance. One iron ingot would buy it.

Steve handed over the extra ingot they'd picked up and secured a place to stay.

Alex washed her hair and stood in the doorway, looking around.

“The house is one thing.”

Steve nodded.

“That doesn't settle what happened earlier.”

Steve nodded again.

## Two Kinds of Welcome Gift

They took off their traveling equipment and had just settled the house when a villager arrived with gifts. Pangpang was the first to say thank you.

He found himself holding a gold ingot.

Steve and Alex each received something too.

Chicken manure.

Delivered straight at them.

Both stood still. Pangpang looked at his own hand, uncertain whether to smile.

Steve asked, “Are you sure you brought the right things?”

The villager waved and left.

The pattern remained remarkably consistent.

Some gave Pangpang iron ingots; others gave him gold. No diamonds, no rare treasures. Just those two things.

But whenever a villager turned to Steve and Alex, the offering changed to chicken manure.

Alex's newly washed clothes needed washing again.

Pangpang set the gold and iron on the table and counted them.

Steve stood opposite him.

“Let's not count our share.”

## Two Ways to Handle Complaints

At first, the two complained quietly.

Then their voices grew louder.

“We all just moved here. Why does only he get the good stuff?”

“You don't have to give us anything. Could you at least stop throwing it at our heads?”

The mayor arrived.

Steve quickly explained what had happened. Alex pointed at the clothes awaiting another wash; the evidence was close at hand.

The mayor listened and frowned at them for a moment.

Then he beckoned to the iron golem.

“Send these two back.”

Steve thought he meant their newly purchased house. He pointed beside him.

“We live right—”

He didn't finish.

The village's custom guard was very efficient at this sort of thing. Both were returned to their original spawn point. Their new house, meanwhile, stayed behind.

Steve stood on the familiar grass without speaking for a long time.

Alex looked at the distant road.

“We bought the house.”

“Yes.”

“They returned the people.”

## The Gentle Half of the Conversation

Back in the village, the mayor had changed his tone.

He took out a golden apple and placed it in Pangpang's hands.

“Those two won't behave. You stay here and eat. Let them reflect on it.”

Pangpang sat on the bench holding the apple. He didn't bite it right away.

He looked at the gold and iron beside him, then at the empty house they'd just bought.

The mayor was still waiting for him to eat.

“Shouldn't I save them some this time?” Pangpang asked.

“They need to reflect on their behavior.”

Pangpang moved over a little, leaving two places for his friends.

The mayor smiled pleasantly.

But before anyone could sit in those two places, they would have to walk all the way back to the village.
