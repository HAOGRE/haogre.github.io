Steve walked into the kitchen and found Pangpang stuffing filling into a round of dough.

“It’s leaking.”

“I’m not done yet.”

Pangpang pinched the last gap shut and set the little dough parcel in the steamer.

Steve walked around to look from the other side.

“So you’ve put the meal inside the bread?”

## No Verdict Yet

Ever since they’d opened the courtyard gate, their shared world—the one with the cooking mod—had been getting quite a few visits from villagers.

Today, Pangpang wanted to try a new recipe.

Bao. Steamed buns.

Alex lifted the cloth over the bowl, saw that the dough had grown since she last checked, and covered it again.

“You’re sure it doesn’t need to go in the oven?”

“We’re steaming it today.”

Steve stared at the steamer. This seemed a little risky. At least with bread, you could see what color it was when it came out of the oven. With the bao hidden under that lid, who knew what was going on in there?

Pangpang handed him a small piece of dough.

“If you’re worried, help.”

Steve’s first one leaked through the bottom.

His second one closed at the top. The filling stayed outside.

Alex turned it over to inspect it.

“This one’s more honest.”

## It Was Only Meant to Be Breakfast

When the lid came off the first batch, Steve was the first to crane his neck.

Plump white bao crowded together. Even those crooked pleats from earlier looked respectable now.

He picked up the one he’d made and broke it open.

There really was filling inside.

Steam rose straight up.

“Were you worried it would run off?” Alex asked.

Steve didn’t answer. He blew on it twice, took a bite, then looked down at it again.

Alex tried one too.

The kitchen went quiet.

Pangpang waited for them to say something. When Steve reached for a second bao, he shifted the steamer a little farther away.

“Tell me if they’re any good first.”

“Don’t move that yet.”

A cough came from outside the window.

A villager stood at the courtyard gate, carrying the things he usually traded for bread.

“What do you want for one of those?”

Another villager stood behind him.

Farther back, someone had already brought a plate.

Pangpang looked at the steamer, then at the gate.

“How about we all make a meal together?”

## Tables First, Then Food

What had started as breakfast for three gradually moved out into the village square.

Villagers brought flour and vegetables. Some carried pots, others stools. Steve turned the millstone, Alex helped mix the filling, and Pangpang taught everyone how to pinch the bao shut.

The blacksmith finished shaping one and set it on the table.

Very solid.

As if he planned to use it to plug a mine tunnel.

Pangpang took a look. He offered no criticism, just gave him a little less filling.

Tables were pushed together into one long row. The first steamer had barely reached the table before someone was carrying over the second.

A villager who grew wheat finished a bao, looked at the buns, then looked over at his wheat field.

“Wheat from that same field?”

Pangpang nodded.

The villager took another, apparently still needing to check.

Steve finally sat down. The bao in his hand was no longer too hot to eat.

“I haven’t even finished breakfast.”

Alex pointed to the sun overhead.

“It’s lunch now.”

## Leave the Long Table for Now

Later, Pangpang was back in the kitchen, rolling out dough.

Steve was quite calm when he saw the long strands of noodles on the board.

He’d already seen vegetables hidden inside dough.

Then Pangpang poured tomato and meat sauce over the cooked noodles.

Alex tried a bite first, then handed Steve a fork.

“Try it yourself.”

Steve twirled a forkful, bent his head to eat it, and quietly pulled the plate closer.

Pangpang held the edge of the plate.

“Spaghetti. We could invite everyone to try this too—”

Steve was already heading out of the courtyard.

“Where are you going?”

“To get the tables.”

The second feast got started faster than the first. The villagers knew to bring plates, the blacksmith knew to bring a pot, and Steve knew he’d have to do some work before he could eat.

As they tidied the square that evening, someone lifted one end of the long table and asked where to put it back.

Pangpang was wiping down the board. When he heard everyone asking what he’d cook next, he thought for a moment.

“There’s something else I want to try—”

The table was set back down.

“Wipe it clean first,” Alex said. “It can stay here.”
