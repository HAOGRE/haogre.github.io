_He was choosing his landing point. The dragon was still performing its attack._

After changing mods, Steve met another dragon.

It was different from the Ender Dragon. The sheer effort it put into preparing its big attack made him feel he ought to count his supplies again.

But Steve was watching its back.

Fighting the Ender Dragon, he had worked out a routine: use wind charges to get up, stay close to its back, launch higher, and bring the mace down. Spear movement and elytra helped him connect the next pass without drifting too far away.

This dragon had a different name. Its back was still in roughly the same place.

“Let's try it.”

He gripped his Divine Mace and threw a wind charge.

As he rose, the dragon shrank below him. Steve had no time to admire the view. He still had to watch his direction, choose his landing point, and make sure that “about to hit the dragon” did not become “about to hit the ground.”

The first blow came down.

It connected.

Steve could not stop there. Another wind charge sent him upward. The familiar movements joined together: close in, gain height, drop. The opponent was different, but the routine he had practiced still worked.

Second blow.

The dragon was still performing its big attack.

Third blow.

On his way back up, Steve began to feel that something was odd.

He had only meant to see whether the method worked. He had not expected the Divine Mace to hit so hard. The dragon's elaborate attack was still underway, but the fight was nearly over.

The fourth blow landed.

The dragon was gone.

Its big attack had not finished.

Steve landed and stood holding the mace for a moment. Until then, he had been busy finding his next landing point. Suddenly he did not need one. That felt strange.

He looked up, then down at the mace.

“That's it?”

There was nothing left for a fifth blow.

Calling it easy would overlook the wind charges and landing points; none of those could be managed with his eyes shut. Calling it a long battle would also be difficult. He could count every blow on one hand.

He put the Divine Mace away and remembered what he had said at the start: “Let's try it.”

The test had established two things.

The old method worked.

This mace worked rather better than he had expected.
