Before they entered the dungeon, Pangpang repaired his shield twice.

After the first repair, its durability was already full.

The second time, he just checked it again.

Steve was handing out potions nearby, and Alex was topping up her arrows. They had just finished a trial in their three-player modded world. Now they had another problem to deal with.

A hostile player was clearly marked as banned, but their character was still on the test server.

Something had gone wrong with the ban. The character hadn't disappeared as it should have and was stuck deep inside the dungeon.

Pangpang stuffed his spare shield into his backpack too.

“Banned, and still in there.”

Steve nodded.

“They must be tough to beat.”

Alex closed the chest. “Let's go have a look.”

## Where Are the Guards?

The first corridor was quiet.

Steve stopped to listen for a moment, then waved the other two forward.

The second corridor was quiet too.

Shield raised, Pangpang inched around the corner, looking left first, then right.

No one on either side.

At the dungeon entrance, they waited a little longer.

The player's character was right there inside, name still hovering overhead, health bar still visible.

There was nothing around it.

Steve looked at Alex.

Alex looked at Pangpang.

Pangpang turned to check behind them.

“Where are the guards?”

No one answered.

They went in.

There was no ambush, no second monster charging out of nowhere. A few hits emptied the lingering character's health bar, leaving a single diamond on the floor.

Pangpang hadn't lowered his shield an inch.

## Just One

Steve picked up the diamond.

Alex waited a few seconds.

Pangpang waited a few more.

Nothing else dropped.

“One?” Pangpang asked.

Steve opened his hand.

One diamond. There was no mistaking it.

Pangpang finally lowered his shield.

“Not even a guard.”

Alex put away the arrows she hadn't fired. “You've been looking for one since the second corridor.”

“I thought there'd be one.”

“Well, now you know.”

The three of them retraced their steps.

Around the first corner, nothing happened.

Around the second, still nothing.

Only when sunlight reached the toes of his boots did Pangpang take one last look back.

The dungeon hadn't collapsed.

No one was chasing them.

And nobody came running out to tell them they'd skipped a stage.

Steve sat on a rock, opened his backpack, and began sorting the potions back into their usual places.

Pangpang ran a hand over his spare shield, still at full durability.

“If I'd known—”

“Keep it for now,” Alex said.

Pangpang nodded and fastened his backpack.

Steve put the diamond away.

This time, the backpack was a little heavier than when they'd set out.

Only a little.
