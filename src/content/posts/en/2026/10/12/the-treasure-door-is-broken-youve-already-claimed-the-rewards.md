Alex was dismantling her Inkcrystal Sword while Pangpang polished his own.

“Yesterday you said that would last you ages.”

“Yesterday I hadn't found this.”

She set the Sea God's Trident beside the crafting table. The water on its tips had dried, but Riptide could still launch her all the way to the cave entrance.

This was a separate modded world the three of them had started. Here, the Sea God's Trident didn't care about the weather or whether there was any water around. You could fly with it from dry ground. Alex had found it while following an ore vein, tested its stats, then broken her old sword down into materials and tucked them into her backpack.

Pangpang looked at the sword in his hands.

He hadn't found a “this” yet.

## Down the Chimney

A sign stood by the sea.

> This underground chimney leads to the hidden realm trials.

Steve held up his torch and studied it.

“Someone built the entrance as a chimney.”

Pangpang peered down. “At least we don't need to find a key.”

They dug footholds into the rock walls and worked their way down. At the bottom, sure enough, the entrance to the trials glowed ahead.

The first challenge was a diamond-headed giant.

Three golden mobs clung to its shoulders, each carrying a bow.

Pangpang had just counted the third when the first elemental arrow struck beside him. Stone chips sprayed his face, and a chunk vanished from his long health bar.

“Over three hundred health,” he said, ducking behind a pillar, “and I still can't afford this.”

Steve switched to his Inkcrystal Bow and aimed at the giant's shoulders. Alex used her trident to swing around to the other side, drawing the giant's attention away. Pangpang waited for the arrows to miss, then leaned out from behind the pillar to get a few hits in.

Only after picking off the three archers, one by one, could they finally hold their ground against the giant.

The last arrow hit. The diamond head crashed to the floor.

Pangpang made a careful sweep for drops.

“That was only the first round,” he said. “Imagine the chest at the end.”

## This One Isn't a Horse

The second boss's name included the Chinese character for “horse.”

Pangpang raised his shield and scanned the ground for hooves.

There were no hooves.

A hulking Gama, wreathed in malice, squeezed out of the shadows and opened its mouth to release a swarm of smaller mobs. Before Pangpang could adjust his position, Alex's trident was already clanging under the blows.

She took a step back and immediately checked its durability.

She'd only just switched to it.

It had nearly become scrap.

Steve and Pangpang hurried to hold off the smaller mobs while Alex used Riptide to put some distance between herself and the fight. They separated the crowded enemies, cleared the nearest ones, then went back to the big one. It took several rounds of this before that dark mass of malice finally dispersed.

One line in the loot's description was especially clear.

Bonus attack damage: 54.

Pangpang looked at the item, then at the trident.

“Nearly broke the thing. Now it has to give it an upgrade, too.”

Alex put the material away.

“Durability is a separate issue.”

## The King Has Three Heads

The third challenge: King Guoulik.

Pangpang heard “king” and looked for a throne.

A shadow fell over them.

Three dragon heads lowered at once, each glowing with a different element.

Steve pulled him behind a stone pillar. Dragon breath swept across the spot where they'd been standing, leaving nowhere safe to set foot.

“You can stop looking for a seat,” Steve said.

The three-headed dragon wouldn't let them take their time separating targets as they had in the previous fight. Whenever one side left an opening, an attack from the other side filled it.

Steve used his bow to make it turn its heads. Pangpang stayed by the pillar, calling out where the attacks would land. Alex tightened her grip on the trident, waited for two streams of dragon breath to cross, then used Riptide to shoot through the gap between them.

The first time, she missed the opening.

The second time, Steve fired his arrow earlier.

At last, they managed to stagger the three heads' attacks. Little by little, they found moments to strike. By the time the last sliver of the health bar vanished, Pangpang's shield needed repairs too.

A few dragon crystals and a sigil lay on the ground.

At the far end of the cavern stood a great door.

The three of them looked at it. Finally, they thought. This had to be it.

## There Really Is Something Wrong with the Door

Steve placed the sigil on it.

The door didn't move.

Alex tried turning it another way.

The door still didn't move.

Pangpang freed both hands, braced himself against the door, and pushed for a good while. The door stayed shut. His boots left two drag marks across the floor.

“Is there a fourth round?”

Something sounded overhead.

All three raised their weapons.

A sign dropped down.

> The door is broken. There is no hidden treasure inside. Opening it won't change that.

Before Pangpang finished reading, a second sign popped up beside it.

> The items dropped by the monsters are your rewards for these trials.

Steve looked down at the ore he'd collected along the way.

Alex felt for the upgrade material she'd put away.

Pangpang tipped a few things out of his backpack. Dragon crystals sat on top of supplies, which sat on top of another bundle of drops. It took him a while to find his last piece of bread.

The three of them stood in silence for a moment.

“So just now,” Pangpang said, pointing at the door, “we were asking it to pay out again?”

Steve put the sigil away. “Let's go.”

They reached the surface just as the sun was setting over the sea.

Alex used Riptide to reach the shore first. Steve climbed out after her, then turned to find Pangpang still carrying the sign.

“What's that for?”

“Reward instructions.”

Pangpang slid the wooden sign into the side of his backpack.

“This time, we're taking everything.”
