By the time Pangpang turned the sign over, Steve was already lying down.

They had brought the sign back from the previous trial. The front explained that the treasure door was broken. In one corner on the back was another line of small print.

Coordinates for two more trials.

Pangpang held it under a torch and wiped it with his sleeve. Once he was sure, he reached over and nudged Steve.

“Get up.”

“Is the door fixed?”

“No. Found two more.”

## First Stop: Two Empty Rooms

They were still in the new modded world the three of them had started together. Alex brought her Sea God's Trident, Steve repaired his Inkcrystal Bow, and Pangpang patched up his shield. They followed the coordinates to the second trial.

The first round had no monsters.

The three of them edged in along the wall and waited a while.

Nothing stirred.

Pangpang pushed open the treasury door.

Nothing in there, either.

“At least the sign was honest,” he said. “Didn't promise there'd be anything.”

The second round was empty, too.

Steve checked the corners. Alex looked up at the vaulted ceiling. Pangpang followed her gaze and held his shield up until his arm began to ache.

Nothing fell.

By the third round, he finally lowered his shield a little.

The ground began to rumble.

A Hinox wreathed in black-and-purple malice charged straight at him. Pangpang barely had time to raise his shield again.

Bam.

The shield was still in his hand.

He was back at the entrance.

Steve hurried over to pull him up while Alex drew the giant away with her trident. Pangpang looked down at the rim of his shield, then back at the spot where he'd been standing.

“Did they pack the first two rounds into this one?”

## If It's Chasing You, Run

They stopped trying to block it.

The arena was big enough, and the stone pillars were still standing. Steve fired an arrow. The Hinox turned toward him, and he immediately ran. Alex landed another arrow from the other side. As soon as the monster went after her, Pangpang leaned out from behind a pillar.

When it was Pangpang's turn to be chased, he ran especially fast.

“Don't you still have a shield?” Steve called.

“I tried that!”

They took turns keeping their distance and firing arrows. Whoever drew the monster's attention put away their bow and concentrated on where they were going.

After enough laps, Pangpang looked at the routes he'd worn across the ground and decided he knew the place well enough to farm it.

At last, the Hinox fell.

They picked up a horn wreathed in malice and rested for a long while before heading out.

Pangpang turned the sign over.

“One more.”

This time, Steve didn't get up right away.

## Second Stop: Two More Empty Rooms

The first round of the third trial was empty.

Pangpang said nothing.

The second round was empty, too.

Pangpang raised his shield.

“Now we know how this works.”

For the final round, Steve made a point of getting everyone into position. They spread out far enough to give each other room, while staying within sight and close enough to help.

From above, they formed a triangle.

Then a black-and-red Lynel charged out, and the triangle was gone.

Steve ended up on the right, Alex on the left, and Pangpang near the exit again.

“It knows too!” Pangpang shouted.

The three of them spread out again. This time, they moved out of its path before it reached them, covering one another as they went.

The Red-Maned Lynel was much more agile than the giant. Steve would spot an opening only for it to turn toward him. Alex used Riptide to get clear, while Pangpang only dared land a few hits as it recovered from an attack.

When they finally brought it down, a curved horn lay on the ground.

Steve opened the trial's bestiary.

Red-Maned Lynel.

The weakest tier of its kind.

Pangpang glanced at his shield.

“Even the weakest one takes this long.”

“Then the strongest—” Steve began, but hoofbeats sounded from deeper inside.

All three turned.

It still wasn't the strongest one.

It was the second tier: a Red-Maned Lynel in armor.

Pangpang drew a breath.

“That's a relief.”

He paused, then added the rest.

“A small one.”

The extra armor kept them busy for another stretch, but at least they remembered how to work together. They aimed for the gaps in its armor and took turns falling back. Eventually, they left the second one on the arena floor, too.

This time, Steve put the bestiary away without turning another page.

Alex counted arrows. Pangpang counted their remaining supplies.

“Still looking for the third tier?”

Steve pointed toward the exit.

“Crafting table first.”

On the way back, the two empty rooms were still quiet.

Pangpang even leaned in for another look as they passed.

“Now I get it,” he said. “These are here so we can catch our breath.”
