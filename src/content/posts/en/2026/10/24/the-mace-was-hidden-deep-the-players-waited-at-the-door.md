Steve fitted the last cobblestone block into place and dusted off his hands.

“Nobody can see it now.”

He had hidden a mace.

The three friends had mined into a trial chamber. This was the same custom world with falling supplies and persistent pursuers, and its vault loot had been changed too: inside a chest was a piece of wood, with a mace fitted inside it.

Pangpang turned the wood over twice.

“A chest outside, another casing inside.”

Steve took the mace, tested its weight, and decided to hide it at the very bottom of the chamber until they had better equipment.

## One more layer

They sealed the hiding place and left.

The player in netherite armor came in afterward, found it, and took the mace.

When the friends returned, the stone seal was broken. The space behind it was empty.

Pangpang looked from the chest outside to the hole at the back.

“Out of a chest and into the stone. Now even the stone hasn’t lasted.”

Steve had no mace to put away. He withdrew his empty hand.

“Let’s go to the Nether first.”

## The door opened, and so did the return trip

They wanted to mine materials. They had barely crossed the Nether portal when they saw dozens of players in netherite armor.

The entrance was crowded.

Before they could see the path beyond, all three were back at spawn.

Alex decided to try elsewhere.

This time, they found another entrance.

As soon as it opened, more than twenty netherite players walked out.

Pangpang had not finished turning around before their return trip was over again.

Standing at the familiar spawn point, Steve finally spoke.

“Have we been opening the same door?”

“Fewer people this time,” said Pangpang.

“Enough,” said Alex.

## The first set came from a mistake

Later, they spotted a small ender pearl contraption high above the ground.

A player in netherite armor dismantled it and prepared to jump down. He had a water bucket, but missed the water landing.

The player went back to spawn. His equipment stayed below.

The three walked over and picked up one complete set of netherite armor.

Pangpang counted carefully.

“One set.”

This time, nobody called one set three.

Farther along, they managed to defeat two more netherite players. At last they found a way through and slipped into the Nether.

The purple portal light settled behind them.

They paused. Nobody moved forward immediately.

Pangpang whispered,

“Why aren’t we back yet?”

Alex nudged him farther inside.

“Because we got in.”
