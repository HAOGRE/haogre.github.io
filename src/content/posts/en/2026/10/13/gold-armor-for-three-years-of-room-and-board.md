Pangpang had barely stepped into the inn when a blast went off outside.

He stopped in the doorway.

“We haven't even ordered.”

A second blast rattled the window frames. A little soup sloshed out of a bowl on the table.

Alex set down her bag. Steve had already grabbed his weapon.

A Golden Lynel stood in the backyard.

## First, Fight for Dinner

This was their save with the monster trial mod installed. The three of them had just returned from a dungeon. All they wanted was a hot meal, a chance to restock, and some rest.

The Golden Lynel wasn't waiting for them to rest.

Pangpang raised his shield and took a hit. His boots carved two furrows through the mud. Steve circled around and struck its armor with his sword. Plenty of noise. Very little effect.

Alex used her trident to put some distance between herself and the Lynel, looking for an opening between the plates.

The Lynel turned faster than they'd expected.

They had to take turns covering one another. Whenever someone got pinned against a wall, another would draw its attention from the side. Every brief chance to catch their breath ended with another attack to fend off.

By the time the Golden Lynel finally fell, the backyard fence barely looked like a fence.

Pangpang didn't look like someone who could stand up on his own, either.

He leaned against a wooden stump and spent a long time catching his breath.

“Three years' worth of strength,” he said. “Just used it all up.”

Steve rested his sword across his knees.

“Who left that thing here?”

Nobody knew.

It had left plenty of loot, though. Especially those thick, gleaming gold armor plates.

Pangpang tried lifting one, then set it back down.

“How are we supposed to carry these?”

## The Innkeeper Checks the Material First

The innkeeper came out carrying a teapot. He looked at the broken fence, then at the trampled hay rack.

Finally, he crouched beside the armor plates.

Ran a hand over one.

Turned it over.

Checked the item description.

“Pure gold.”

Pangpang looked up.

“It isn't just gold-colored.” The innkeeper tapped the plate. “It's made of pure gold.”

These were the modded monster's own heavy armor plates, tough enough to have given them all that trouble. Now that the plates were off the monster, the inventory helpfully listed the material.

Steve sat up a little straighter.

“So we can sell them?”

“Yes.”

Pangpang tried lifting a plate again.

“But what about the transport costs?”

The innkeeper looked at the gold, then at the three adventurers' luggage.

“I'll take it. In exchange, I'll cover your travel, meals, and lodging for three years. How's that?”

All three went quiet.

Pangpang had been rubbing his arm. Even his hand stopped.

“All three of us?”

“All three.”

“Three years?”

The innkeeper nodded.

Steve immediately put down the plate he'd been about to stuff into his bag.

“Deal.”

The innkeeper called his staff over to move the plates.

Pangpang watched them work for a while, then finally braced himself against the stump and stood up.

“Spent three years' worth of strength back there.”

Alex looked at him.

“Now we've got three years' worth of meals back.”

## One Thing They Hadn't Checked

On his way to the kitchen, Pangpang spotted something else among the drops.

Lynel meat, from the mod.

He tasted a small piece, paused, then hurried off to find the innkeeper with the rest.

“Can you make a stew with this?”

A pot of stew arrived at the table. At last, the three of them had a hot meal.

Steve finished a bowl and casually opened his stats panel.

Then he put the bowl down.

His maximum stamina had increased by more than three hundred points.

Alex checked her own panel.

Pangpang was about to help himself to a second bowl. When he saw their expressions, his hand slowed.

“What is it?”

Steve turned the panel toward him.

Pangpang's eyes widened.

“What kind of meat is this?”

The innkeeper served himself a bowl and sat down to try it.

For a while, the only sound at the table was spoons touching bowls.

After a long silence, Steve sighed.

“How many Lynels did we fight in the earlier trials?”

Pangpang started counting on his fingers.

“What did we take?” Steve asked.

“Horns, materials…”

Pangpang's hand stopped in midair.

They hadn't taken the armor.

Or the meat.

Alex pushed the soup toward them.

“Let's eat this meal first.”

Steve took out his little notebook and added two items beneath their supply list.

Armor.

Meat.

Pangpang leaned over to read, then pointed to the top of the page.

“First, write ‘Make sure we can win.’”

Steve added that before picking up his spoon again.
