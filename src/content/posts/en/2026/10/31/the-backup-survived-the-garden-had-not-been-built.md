Steve placed the last block on the roof's edge and stepped back.

Crooked.

He climbed up, removed it, placed it again, and stepped back once more.

That was better.

In this separate hardcore world, he had built a garden modeled on the Old Summer Palace, block by block. Pond banks, covered walkways, pavilions: he had gathered and placed everything himself. No command had paved the ground for him. Nobody had finished half the garden while he slept.

He stood at the entrance for a long time.

“Just a little building project.”

Then he checked his tools' durability and decided that was enough of a sentence.

## A Little Practice Before the Tour

With the garden finished, Steve felt like doing something else.

Practicing with explosives, for example.

He found a spot and started placing things. Even as he worked, he was thinking about that roof. Next time, he would look at it from the other side of the pond. The reflection ought to be good, too.

He did not keep a proper count of what he was putting down.

When the first blast went off, he was still observing the result.

By the time the others went off, the result was paying his garden a visit.

Dirt flew across the bank. Water poured through a gap. A section of walkway disappeared, followed by the section beside it. The roof Steve had adjusted so carefully no longer presented a problem of symmetry.

Both sides were gone.

When the smoke cleared, the entrance was still in its original direction.

It just wasn't particularly necessary anymore. You could enter from anywhere.

Steve stood still for a long time.

## Fortunately, There Was a Backup

Suddenly, he looked up.

“There's a backup.”

He said it quietly. The second time was louder.

“I have a backup.”

A moment earlier, all he had was rubble. Now he had a file he could open. Steve quit, found the save, and carefully checked its name.

The right world.

The backup was there. Undamaged.

He loaded it.

Daylight. Neat grass. The old tree still stood beside the pond, with a patch of shade beneath its leaves.

No smoke. No crater. No shattered walkway.

No walkway, either.

Steve took a few steps forward and stopped where he had planned the entrance. Grass blocks lay beneath his feet, one after another, so perfectly intact that there was nothing to complain about.

At last, he remembered when he had made this backup.

Before he started building.

_The backup preserved the world, just before the first brick._

Steve opened his inventory, then closed it.

He knew this clearing very well. He had stood right here at the beginning, admiring the pond and the tree, thinking how good a covered walkway would look beside them.

The pond was still very good.

So was the tree.

He looked at the tool in his hand, raised it a little, then lowered it again.

“Right.”

After a while, he finished the thought.

“Excellent restoration of the grass.”
