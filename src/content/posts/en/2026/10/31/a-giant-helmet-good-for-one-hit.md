Steve laid the gun on his crafting table and checked the recipe again.

This world had firearm and giant-monster mods installed, and the M7's price was suitably monstrous. The gun needed materials. The ammunition needed more. A pile of minerals went into a recipe and came out looking very small.

The night before, he had put one stack of rotten flesh into a furnace, hoping for some diamonds.

This mod allowed rotten flesh to smelt into all sorts of random things.

Now he had a gun.

He did not have ammunition.

Steve picked it up, inspected it, and put it down again.

Impressive to hold. For fighting monsters, he might as well take the crafting table.

## Three More Stacks

He fed another three stacks of rotten flesh into the furnace.

This time, he counted sixty blue-tier rounds in the output.

Steve checked their color without getting excited. In this world's equipment rankings, blue was nowhere near the top.

Then he read the description.

Armor-piercing.

He read that twice and decided to test them.

His test subject was a titan zombie.

It stood in the distance under an enormous iron helmet. Steve looked up at it for a while, then down at his gun.

“Perfect.”

He had come to test ammunition. Now he was thinking about the helmet, too.

## A Thorough Job

One magazine later, the titan zombie was down.

Steve did not need a second round of firing.

He walked over, checked the drops, then looked up at the space the enormous creature had occupied. Perhaps blue was an unnecessarily modest color.

The giant iron helmet had dropped as well.

He hurried to pick it up.

Big. Heavy. The sort of trophy that deserved its own display space.

Then he checked the durability.

One.

Steve said nothing. He turned the helmet around, as though looking from another side might improve the number.

Still one.

At last, he understood. That magazine had not been having a conversation only with the zombie.

It had been speaking to the helmet the whole time.

“Armor-piercing.”

He repeated the words, considerably more quietly now.

## There Was a Little One, Too

Steve put on his new helmet. Before he could take it home and deal with it properly, quick footsteps sounded near his boots.

A baby zombie rushed at him.

Compared with the previous one, it hardly justified looking up.

It hit him once.

The helmet broke.

Steve remained standing, suddenly lighter around the head. The baby zombie was still beside him, entirely unaware that it had accomplished something significant.

_The big monster dropped the helmet. The little one finished the job._

He looked down at the metal pieces, then at the M7 in his hand.

The gun was fine.

The ammunition's effect had been demonstrated.

He had, undeniably, obtained a titan's exclusive helmet on this trip.

“The fitting session,” Steve said, “was a little short.”
