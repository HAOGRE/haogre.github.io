The titan zombie had dropped a stack of netherite ingots as well as its helmet.

Steve stared at them and thought about the firearm mod's red-tier ammunition.

Everyone called those rounds “miracle pills.”

He had thought the name exaggerated. Then he opened the recipe and decided it was quite restrained.

Two stacks of diamonds. Two stacks of iron ingots. One stack of netherite ingots.

For one round.

Steve had expected to make sixty. His hand stopped halfway to the crafting grid, and he checked the output again.

Definitely one.

## The Giant Was Gone. So Were the Materials.

This was still the same world full of custom monsters and firearms. Steve took his new round to test on a chaos ender giant.

He fired.

The giant was gone.

Steve stayed where he was, looking first at the monster, then at the gun.

“Enough damage.”

After a moment, he added something.

“There goes a stack of netherite.”

He hurried over to search the drops, hoping to find the giant's exclusive token.

No token.

There was a full stack of nether stars, though.

That restored his enthusiasm. He brought out his stored iron blocks and started building beacons. One went up, then another. Beams of light rose into the sky, and the camp looked increasingly impressive.

When he went to replenish his ammunition, he discovered that he had built the budget into the ground.

The beacons were lit.

The gun was empty.

Steve took the M7 to his secret base, down its long water elevator, and put it in the chest at the bottom.

Before closing the lid, he gave the gun one last look.

“Have a rest.”

## An Old Acquaintance in a Chest

Later, hundreds of players in netherite armor came after him.

Steve ran to his secret base to replenish his supplies. He opened the chest and met the gun again.

This time, he had just enough materials to make forty-five miracle rounds.

Exactly one full magazine.

He loaded them and paused.

The noise outside did not allow much time for arithmetic.

So he fired all forty-five rounds.

By the time he could look down at the empty magazine, every number he had postponed came back at once.

Forty-five stacks of netherite ingots.

Ninety stacks of diamonds.

Ninety stacks of iron ingots.

Steve surveyed the loot on the ground. The longer he looked, the less that exchange had felt like fighting back.

It felt like moving his warehouse outdoors.

## Three Boxes

He found three shulker boxes.

The first was full of totems of undying.

So was the second.

And the third.

Steve crouched beside them. The running total that had been sinking through his mind suddenly stopped.

He had searched every part of this little hundred-by-hundred map without finding a woodland mansion. The totems he wanted always seemed to belong to somebody else. His own storage slots stayed empty.

Now he had three full boxes.

_Count ammunition in materials. Count totems in time spent searching._

Steve looked at the empty magazine again.

Expensive. Still expensive.

Forty-five stacks of netherite would not magically return to storage because he had found something good.

But he closed the three shulker boxes far more gently than before.

“Next time,” he told the M7, “let me check their inventory before I fire.”

The gun said nothing.

Fortunately, silence did not cost ammunition.
