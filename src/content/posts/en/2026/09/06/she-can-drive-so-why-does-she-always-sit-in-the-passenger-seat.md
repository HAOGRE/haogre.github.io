My wife can drive, but she likes sitting in the passenger seat. I like sitting there too. The difference is that I sit there because I can't drive, while she sits there because she can.

So whenever we get in the car, we both make a perfectly coordinated move toward the passenger seat. Who will actually drive usually remains unresolved for the day.

## She Can Drive, but She Just Won't

Yesterday I went with her to practice driving. She got into the driver's seat, familiarized herself with the car, and started driving slowly. After about an hour, I figured she was already driving like a veteran.

Reversing, turning, and pulling away were all fine. She handled the car smoothly. From where I was sitting, she had reached the point where I could safely trust her with my life.

Yet after practice, she still wouldn't drive.

I couldn't understand it. She could drive, and she had gotten pretty good at it. Why did she still refuse to take the wheel? Then I realized the problem might not be her ability. It might be the seat.

The passenger seat is simply too comfortable. Someone else watches the road and handles the driving, while you only have to sit there. You can chat, look out the window, or even offer directions that may or may not help when the driver reaches a complicated intersection.

Driving is different. Your hands stay on the wheel, your eyes stay on the road, and you have to brake in time for traffic lights. The passenger only needs to ask, “Are we turning up ahead?” The driver has to turn that question into an actual maneuver.

## The Lure-and-Pity Routine

Since reasoning wasn't working, I had to try a little strategy.

I told her, “Then I'll walk home. If you don't drive us back, you'll have to figure out the subway yourself.”

Then I left.

This was part lure, part playing the victim. The plan was simple: leave the scene, leave her beside the car, and make her realize she had only two choices: drive home or figure out her own transportation.

She didn't call, even after I'd walked a long way.

At that point, I was starting to panic. I kept walking and wondered whether I should call her first to ask what we were supposed to do now. Then I reconsidered. I'd already made my declaration, so I couldn't be the one to back down. I turned around and started walking back, while pretending I was almost at the subway station.

Before long, she called and asked, “What's wrong?”

I still tried to sound calm. “Nothing. I'm almost at the subway station.”

In reality, I was nowhere near the subway station.

## Back in the Passenger Seat

After some arguing and a bit of back-and-forth, we finally reached a result.

I ended up back in the passenger seat, and she drove us home.

That proved she was perfectly capable of driving. The car drove just fine, nothing went wrong on the way, and nothing happened that required me to get out and walk home.

So all those earlier explanations, “I can't drive,” “I'm afraid to drive,” and “I need more practice,” were probably just surface excuses. The real reason was likely that she simply likes sitting in the passenger seat.

I think I understand the situation now. The passenger seat looks like just another chair in the car, but it offers comfort, freedom, and the small pleasure of handing responsibility to someone else. Once you're sitting there, the driver, the route, and when to brake are temporarily someone else's problem.

The passenger seat has one more major advantage: no matter how the drive turns out, you can always offer a few comments from the side.

## More Research into the Thirty-Six Stratagems

This worked out well, but she still has some way to go before she becomes a true veteran driver.

She has already proved that she can drive. The next challenge is getting her to want to take the driver's seat.

Looks like I'll have to keep working through the Thirty-Six Stratagems. I've already used the lure and the pity play. Next time, I may need something gentler, ideally a method that gets her to pick up the keys without starting a family argument.

Of course, the ideal outcome is for her to become a true veteran driver while I keep sitting in the passenger seat.

After all, I can't drive, but I am very good at sitting in the passenger seat.
