Steve, Alex and Pangpang woke up without any ground beneath them.

They were standing on a newly generated sky island. It was tiny: one tree, a few blocks of dirt, and a chicken pecking seriously at empty air. Clouds stretched in every direction. Faraway islands looked as if someone had scattered blocks across the sky.

Pangpang looked at the void, then at the single piece of bread in his backpack.

“Do you think our base will stay up this time?”

“Only if you don’t mine the last block of dirt,” said Steve.

Alex unfolded a map and placed a blue amulet between them. Strange dragon letters appeared on its surface:

> After one hundred days, the sky will choose what remains.

They did not know what the sentence meant. They only knew that every new world began with cutting trees, building a shelter, and waiting for a creeper to perform an unscheduled structural inspection.

## A Base Above the Clouds

They spent three days widening the island.

Steve built the walls, Alex organized the storage and farms, and Pangpang carried materials. He carried them very seriously, although he stopped in the middle of every bridge to count his toes and make sure he was still on the island.

On the fourth night, a creeper climbed over the cloud bridge. It studied the sign that said “Monsters not allowed,” then looked at Pangpang.

“If you can read, you know you’re not welcome here,” Pangpang said.

The creeper hissed.

“It may not be able to read,” said Steve.

“Then it should at least understand the picture.”

The explosion removed one wall, one of Pangpang’s shoes, and nothing from the sign. Alex turned the sign around and wrote: “A sign cannot stop a creeper. Leave an exit.”

By day ten, they had a warehouse, a farm and a defensive wall. Every night the amulet flashed, pointing west through forests and ice fields toward a volcano.

“That’s the quest route,” said Steve.

“That’s a tourist route,” said Pangpang. “Quest routes don’t normally go through volcanoes.”

## The Blue Eyes in the Volcano

From day eleven to day thirty, they crossed forests, deserts and an ice field that glowed at night. They found an old ruin and a map showing a dragon. In one corner, it said:

> The frost dragon sleeps inside a crack in the sky. Ten sky feathers can wake it.

The volcano was their first clue.

Smoke rose from the crater in black ribbons. Lava froze into violet glass halfway down the slope. The amulet trembled in Steve’s hand.

Two enormous icy-blue eyes opened inside the smoke.

Pangpang raised his shield. “That does not look like a dragon that wants to be woken.”

The eyes vanished. A column of frost shot into the sky, freezing the clouds into a staircase. A single feather drifted down beside the amulet.

It was the first sky feather.

## The Ocean Tyrant and the Swimming Chest

The second marker lay beneath the sea. They prepared water-breathing potions, then spent half a day arguing about why Pangpang had put bread inside his helmet.

The ocean tyrant emerged from a sunken temple, its tail stirring up a cloud of silt. Steve blocked its first attack with a trident. Alex fired through the bubbles. Pangpang hid behind a treasure chest while insisting he was protecting it.

When the tyrant broke a pillar, Pangpang was swept away, still holding the chest.

“Can you swim?” Alex called.

“I can float!” he answered.

At the end, Steve lured the tyrant toward a water mechanism, Alex broke the crystal controlling the current, and Pangpang fell from above with the chest. It was not a planned attack, but it worked.

The second feather rose from the chest and revealed a stairway leading upward through the sea.

## Seven Feathers Above the Clouds

On day fifty-one, they climbed the stairs into a chain of floating islands. The bridges were made of wind and changed direction every few seconds.

The third feather hid inside a moving cloud. The fourth was being used as a ball by a group of sky sprites. The fifth grew from an upside-down tree. The sixth rested on a cloud deer’s antler; Alex traded it for apples instead of chasing the animal.

When the seventh feather appeared, thunder rolled through the sky. A golden eagle king burst from the Cloudheart Palace, sinking an island by one block with every wingbeat.

The feathers circled the amulet. New dragon letters appeared:

> The feathers are not showing you the road. You are putting the road back together.

Steve attacked the eagle. Alex searched for the gap in its wings. Pangpang repaired the collapsing bridge block by block, then was blown over the edge by a gust.

“Don’t come after me!” he shouted. “Defeat it first!”

Steve and Alex brought the eagle down. Pangpang swung back from a cloud vine and collided with its chest.

“I’m back!”

“You came back by yourself,” said Steve.

“That is still a tactic.”

Inside the opened palace, they found an ice-blue dragon egg. Its shell already had hairline cracks.

## The Egg Hatches on Day Ninety

From day seventy-one to day eighty-nine, they turned the sky base into a real cloud camp. Alex made sails from the feathers. Steve repaired the bridges. Pangpang labeled every chest: “important,” “very important,” “do not open,” and “Pangpang already opened this.”

On day ninety, a black floating fortress appeared. The Shadow Storm Lord stood above it, six wings covering half the sky. It drew a boundary of black lightning around their base.

That night, the dragon egg cracked.

The three of them sat around the altar. Steve said nothing. Alex turned down the torches. Pangpang prepared an apple, a cake and, at a respectful distance, the last piece of bread.

A tiny frost dragon pushed through the shell. It looked at Alex’s bow, then Pangpang’s cake, and finally touched Steve’s boot with its forehead.

“It chose you as its master,” Pangpang said.

“As a partner,” Steve corrected.

The dragon sneezed. The altar froze. So did Pangpang’s cake.

“It needs training,” said Alex.

“I can teach it not to freeze its friends’ food.”

The dragon sneezed again and turned Pangpang’s coffee into ice cream.

## The Fortress and the Final Core

On day ninety-one, the shadow army crossed the cloud bridges. The frost dragon was still too young to fly high, but it froze one monster’s foot with a tiny breath of ice.

“We have air support!” Pangpang announced.

The dragon sneezed and froze the bridge beneath him.

By day ninety-nine, the base had lost three walls, two chests of potatoes and the sign that said “do not open.” The Shadow End King stepped out of the fortress with a black spear and three dark cores in its chest.

The battle had three stages. Steve held the front line. Alex broke the chains around the cores with her storm bow. Pangpang threw his returning axe, sometimes at the enemy and sometimes at a block he had just placed.

When the six wings opened, the entire sky began to spin. Alex fired a rope arrow and pulled Pangpang back onto the platform. The young dragon flew into the storm and froze it into a bridge.

At last, the final core appeared. The amulet showed one sentence:

> The core is the weakness.

Steve raised the sky sword. Alex drew her final arrow. Pangpang recovered his axe. The frost dragon dived from the highest cloud.

Sword, arrow, axe and blue-white dragon breath struck at once.

The core cracked three times, then shattered. The Shadow End King fell into the clouds and dissolved like black snow.

## Day One Hundred: Homeward

At sunrise, the amulet turned gold.

> Hundred-day trial complete.

The floating islands stabilized. Torches returned. The cloud bridges repaired themselves. Even the potatoes came back to the warehouse.

The frost dragon had grown large enough for all three adventurers. Steve sat in front. Alex checked the route. Pangpang held the last piece of bread and reminded the dragon not to teleport without warning.

The dragon spread its wings.

Far below, they saw their first tiny island. The tree had grown tall. The first broken wall was still there, and the chicken beside it had become surprisingly large.

“Where should we go next?” Alex asked.

Pangpang looked across the clouds. “Home first. We can decide the next place after that.”

Steve nodded.

They flew over the repaired islands, toward the unmarked place on the map.

It was home.

It was also the beginning of the next adventure.
