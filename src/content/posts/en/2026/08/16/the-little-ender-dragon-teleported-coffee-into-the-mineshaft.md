In the blocky continent of Minecraft, Pangpang always considered himself a very patient person.

For example, when mining, he could walk back and forth in the same mineshaft a dozen times until he was sure not a single block of coal was left behind. Or when facing the Ender Dragon, he could be burned back to his spawn point by dragon's breath hundreds of times, only to wake up the next morning still willing to grip his diamond sword again.

Steve had a different perspective on this.

"You're not patient," Steve said. "You just haven't realized how many times you've already failed."

Pangpang didn't argue. He hefted his iron pickaxe onto his shoulder, double-checked the potions, blocks, and food in his inventory, and made sure the little gadget he insisted on calling the "End-Alloy Coffee Portafilter" was still in his pocket.

That portafilter was originally just a normal coffee portafilter that had been roasted shiny by the little Ender Dragon, with exactly 3.618 grams of tin stuck to it. Pangpang firmly believed it now possessed some kind of special ability; Steve believed its only function was being hot to the touch.

They were preparing to head into the mineshaft that day, looking for a clue left by a talking minecart.

But the story starts much earlier than that. If Pangpang hadn't failed hundreds of times in the End, they wouldn't have gotten that dragon egg. And they certainly wouldn't have ended up with that troublesome little dragon who teleported coffee into the mineshaft and derailed everyone's plans.

## After Hundreds of Failures, the Ender Dragon Finally Fell

When Pangpang challenged the Ender Dragon for the first time, he brought an iron sword, two Awkward Potions, and an inventory full of cobblestone.

He thought he was thoroughly prepared.

Standing next to the End Portal, Steve watched Pangpang place the cobblestone on the ground block by block and asked, "How do you plan to fight it?"

"First, build a safe house," Pangpang said.

"There are no roofs in the End."

"That's exactly why I need to build one."

The safe house was only three blocks high when the Ender Dragon swooped down from the distance. Its black wings brushed past the obsidian pillars, and dragon's breath spread across the ground like a purple fog. Just as Pangpang ducked his head behind the cobblestone wall, the breath poured in through the gap in the roof.

He rushed out of the safe house, purple flames still clinging to his boots, and before he could run ten steps, he was swept into the portal by the dragon's tail.

The first challenge was over.

The second time, Pangpang brought more cobblestone.

The third time, he brought a water bucket, planning to extinguish the fire under his feet when the dragon's breath approached.

The fourth time, he forgot the water bucket at camp.

The fifth time, the Ender Dragon smashed his newly built bunker in one breath. Pangpang lifted his head from the rubble, only to see Steve run past him, holding the forgotten bucket of water.

"What are you doing with the water?" Pangpang asked.

Steve looked back. "You said you needed it."

"Then why are you only bringing it now?"

"Because the first few times you died too fast, and I couldn't catch up."

Running back to the End from his spawn point once again, Pangpang decided to start learning from the basics. He observed the Ender Dragon's flight path, memorized the distinct sound just before the dragon's breath landed, and practiced dodging sideways when the black wings dipped low. He also marked the End Crystals on the tall pillars one by one, destroying the easy ones first before using his bow to deal with the ones hidden in iron cages.

His failure count slowly dropped from over a dozen times a day to just two or three.

It wasn't because the Ender Dragon had grown weaker, but because Pangpang finally knew where he was supposed to stand.

The true final battle happened on a moonless night in the End.

Pangpang's diamond armor had only half a durability bar left, and he clutched the last healing potion in his inventory. Steve stood behind another pillar, tasked with keeping an eye on the caged End Crystals. The little Ender Dragon hadn't appeared yet, of course—back then, it was just a dark purple dragon egg resting quietly on the portal.

The Ender Dragon circled back from the distance, a ball of purple light glowing in its mouth.

Pangpang didn't run.

He waited for the exact moment the dragon's breath was about to land, jumped sideways onto a two-block-high platform he had built in advance, and immediately shifted one block to the left upon landing. The purple mist grazed his boots as it fell, and the Ender Dragon crashed into the pillar next to the platform, its wings pausing for a brief second.

"Now!" Steve shouted.

Steve drew his bow fully. The arrow flew across half the End and shattered the final End Crystal. When the crystal exploded, a white light illuminated the dark continent like a brief snowfall.

The Ender Dragon had lost its source of regeneration.

It swooped down once more. Pangpang raised his shield, forcefully blocking the impact of the dragon's head. The collision pushed him back several blocks, his boots leaving two shallow grooves on the end stone. Steve rushed out from the side, his sword slashing at the edge of the dragon's wing, while Pangpang swung his iron pickaxe at its tail.

They didn't execute a single beautiful combo. Pangpang would jump too early out of sheer nervousness, and Steve would step on a block he had just placed at the most critical moment. But the Ender Dragon's health bar slowly chipped away, finally stopping at the very last sliver.

Looking at that sliver of red, Pangpang suddenly realized his palms were covered in sweat.

The Ender Dragon let out a low roar, and its body fractured from head to tail into countless beams of purple and white light, like fireworks rising in reverse. The light rained down on them, and experience orbs cascaded from the sky, making a soft, jingling sound as they hit the ground.

High up on the portal, a dark purple dragon egg was left behind.

Pangpang walked over but didn't reach out immediately. He had failed in the End far too many times; he knew that anything that looked quiet could send a person to the top of a mountain, into a lava lake, or right back to the spawn point in the very next second.

Steve picked up the dragon egg.

Pangpang looked at him and smiled. "That's your souvenir."

Steve asked, "You don't want it?"

"I can look at it," Pangpang said. "But this victory is something we won together."

## The Dragon Egg Teleported to the Mountaintop

After returning to camp, Steve decided to build a display pedestal for the dragon egg.

He picked the flattest stone, surrounded it with four End Rods, and hung a sign behind it:

"Ender Dragon Souvenir. Do Not Touch."

Pangpang stood next to it, reminding him, "Make sure you don't lose it. That cost me hundreds of defeats."

"I know," Steve said.

"Don't mine it."

"I know."

"Don't use a piston on it."

"I know."

Steve placed the dragon egg on the pedestal and immediately turned around to mine a piece of stone next to it.

The dragon egg made a sharp *pop* and instantly vanished.

Pangpang slowly raised his head.

At the top of the mountain, a speck of dark purple light flashed.

"It's okay." Pangpang took a deep breath. "We defeated the Ender Dragon. This little thing won't stump us."

Ten minutes later, they had fallen into caves three times, been shot by skeletons six times, and had half a room blown up by a Creeper. Steve went ahead to find a path up the mountain while Pangpang was tasked with keeping an eye on the dragon egg at the top. But every time Pangpang finally climbed next to it, the dragon egg would teleport to another rock just before he could reach out.

It was like a purple die that refused to be picked up.

Pangpang chased it halfway around the mountain with blocks in hand, finally cornering it at the top of a tree. He first built a three-layer wall around it, then laid a safe path from the treetop all the way back to camp, and finally placed the dragon egg into a chest lined with wool.

Steve looked at the chest. "Didn't you say you were going to build a display pedestal for it?"

"The pedestal can wait," Pangpang said. "Right now, we need anti-escape facilities."

Just as they were about to head back to camp, the dragon egg wobbled slightly.

Both of them took three steps back simultaneously.

Pangpang gripped his iron pickaxe tightly. "It's not going to hatch, is it?"

Steve drew his iron sword. "If it hatches, we'll name it Little End."

The dragon egg wobbled again.

Pangpang immediately corrected him. "No, we'll name it 'Please Don't Hit Me Again'."

A low dragon roar echoed from the dark sky in the distance.

Pangpang and Steve looked at each other.

Then Pangpang's stomach growled.

Steve put his sword away. "Let's eat first, hero."

Pangpang nodded. "Sure, but no eggs today."

After returning to camp, Pangpang divided the food into three portions. Steve took the bread, Pangpang took a baked potato, and the dragon egg stayed in the chest, temporarily receiving no food at all.

"It hasn't hatched yet," Steve said.

"I know." Pangpang stuffed a huge bite of potato into his mouth. "But it never hurts to prepare in advance."

"What did you prepare?"

Pangpang pulled out a piece of paper from his inventory. On it was a long list of plans: where the little Ender Dragon would sleep, what it would eat, whether it could be taken into the Nether, who would be responsible for putting out fires when it breathed fire, and most importantly, a rule stating that the little Ender Dragon was not allowed to teleport Pangpang's coffee.

After reading it, Steve said, "Did you forget to ask if it even wants to live here?"

Pangpang flipped the paper over. "We'll discuss that item after it's born."

At night, the camp grew quiet. The torches flickered on the walls, and the chest next to the display pedestal made no sound. Steve dozed off leaning against the bed, while Pangpang lay over the table, earnestly researching how to install a lock on a chest for an egg that could teleport.

Just as he finished drafting his third lock design, a very faint *crack* came from the chest.

Pangpang looked up.

A thin fissure had opened on the dragon egg.

## It Hatched, But First It Roasted the Coffee Portafilter

The next morning, just as Pangpang had finished brewing a cup of coffee, the dragon egg cracked open.

The fissure first emitted a beam of purple light, followed by a second and a third. The eggshell didn't shatter across the floor; instead, as if pushed by something from the inside, the pieces floated into the air before gently landing around the display pedestal.

A little Ender Dragon no bigger than a palm crawled out.

Its wings hadn't fully unfurled yet, but its tail was already quite long. It stared at Steve for three seconds, tilted its head to look at Pangpang for another three, and then lunged into Steve's arms, letting out a milky little cry: "Awoo."

Pangpang froze on the spot.

"It's over," he said. "It thinks Steve is its dad."

Steve held the little dragon and earnestly corrected it, "Partner, not dad."

The little Ender Dragon rubbed its head against his shoulder, looking as though it understood, or perhaps didn't understand at all. Trying to get its attention, Pangpang waved the coffee portafilter in front of it.

The little Ender Dragon opened its mouth and spat a tiny puff of purple fire.

The flame landed squarely on the coffee portafilter.

The portafilter wasn't destroyed; instead, it glowed as brightly as a piece of metal fresh out of a furnace. Pangpang immediately put it on a scale. The numbers ticked from 3.617 to 3.618 grams.

Steve asked, "What is that?"

Pangpang said solemnly, "The legendary End-Alloy Coffee Portafilter."

"What does it do?"

"Drinking coffee made with this might slightly increase mining speed."

"Might?"

"Or it might just be really hot."

The little Ender Dragon flew two circles around them before suddenly barking at a cave in the distance. Low groans of zombies echoed from inside, along with a clinking sound, like someone hammering minecart tracks in the dark.

Pangpang immediately hoisted his iron pickaxe. "A new adventure awaits."

Steve grabbed his sword, and the little Ender Dragon hopped onto his shoulder.

Pangpang added, "Just to be clear: no one is allowed to spill my coffee."

The little Ender Dragon tilted its head.

The next second, the coffee cup vanished from the table and appeared in the deepest depths of the cave.

Pangpang remained silent for three seconds.

"You little dragon," he shouted into the cave, "you're even better at causing trouble than an Enderman!"

And so, the three of them—no, the two of them and one little Ender Dragon—set off toward the mysterious cave.

Along the way, Pangpang kept lecturing it on the responsibilities of a guardian.

"First, no breathing fire whenever you feel like it. Second, no teleporting things away. Third, don't fly too fast; you have to stay where we can see you at the very least."

As the little Ender Dragon flew, it teleported flowers by the road, rocks, and the torches Steve had just placed down to completely different locations.

Steve picked up a displaced flower from the ground. "Is it listening?"

Pangpang said, "It's listening very carefully."

"Then why is it still doing it?"

"Because it's practicing how not to listen."

## The Coffee Exam in the Mineshaft

The cave entrance was narrow, and inside it was as dark as if Pangpang had forgotten to bring torches.

"I brought one!" Pangpang confidently pulled out a torch.

Seeing the torch, the little Ender Dragon suddenly got excited. It opened its mouth and spat a small puff of purple flame. The torch instantly lit up like a miniature sun, illuminating the entire mineshaft in a brilliant purplish-white hue.

Steve covered his eyes. "Is it trying to light the way, or bake us into block-shaped bread?"

Pangpang jammed the torch into the wall. "Brighter is always better."

The moment he spoke, a block of gravel fell from the ceiling and landed right on his helmet.

Steve said, "Brighter also makes it easier for the gravel to see you."

They walked deeper into the cave, and a trail of strange ores gradually appeared on the ground. The ores looked half like diamond and half like tin, radiating a faint purple glow. Pangpang crouched down, studying them for a long time before sketching them on paper and writing three question marks next to it.

"These must be the legendary End Ores," he said.

Steve pointed at the ground next to him. "Check for traps first."

But Pangpang had already swung his pickaxe.

*Click.*

The ground suddenly gave way. Pangpang, Steve, and the little Ender Dragon tumbled down into a deeper cavern, with Pangpang tightly hugging the newly mined ore as he landed.

"I knew it!" he yelled mid-air. "Ores like this are definitely worth a lot!"

Steve fell right past him. "Look at where we're falling first!"

In the center of the cavern sat a stone table.

On the table rested Pangpang's teleported coffee cup, and next to the cup stood a wooden sign:

"Drink the coffee to continue your adventure."

Pangpang scrambled up, looking first at the coffee, then at the sign, and finally at the little Ender Dragon.

"Why is there a coffee exam in a mineshaft?"

The little Ender Dragon flew over to the stone table and sniffed. Without waiting for anyone's permission, it lowered its head and chugged the entire cup of coffee in one gulp.

The next second, purple lightning sparked from its wings. Its body zoomed up into the air with a *whoosh* and slammed right into the ceiling with a *smack*.

Coal, iron ore, and several blocks of the purple ore rained down from above.

Pangpang's eyes lit up. "So coffee really *does* increase mining speed!"

Steve watched the little Ender Dragon, which was now buried in a pile of coal. "I think it increased its wall-smashing speed."

The little Ender Dragon poked its head out of the coal pile and shook off the soot, acting completely unfazed by the fact that it had just crashed headfirst into the ceiling.

Just then, massive footsteps echoed from deep within the cavern.

*Thump.*

*Thump.*

By the third step, the coffee cup on the stone table rattled.

Pangpang gripped his iron pickaxe tightly. "It's not another Ender Dragon, is it?"

A heavy, raspy voice came from the dark:

"I'm not an Ender Dragon."

The voice paused.

"I am the Ender Dragon's cousin, the Ender Lobster."

## The Ender Lobster's Treasure Management System

The creature that emerged from the shadows was indeed a giant purple lobster.

It possessed two enormous claws taller than Pangpang, and its tail dragged along the ground, crushing small stones into dust with every step. Perched lopsidedly on its head was a diamond helmet that was far too small, only managing to cover the centermost patch of its shell.

"Why would a dragon have a lobster cousin?" Pangpang whispered.

"We have too few relatives in the End," the Ender Lobster heard him and turned to answer. "I just claimed the title randomly."

The little Ender Dragon wasn't afraid at all. It flew over and bumped the lobster's claw.

"Awoo."

The Ender Lobster suddenly started crying.

Its tears fell drop by drop from the edge of its helmet, forming purple crystals on the floor.

"This is wonderful," it sniffled. "Finally, someone came to see me. I've waited in this mineshaft for three hundred years, and not even a silverfish would listen to my jokes."

Steve asked, "Why are you here alone?"

"Because I'm in charge of managing the treasure."

"Who put you in charge?"

"No one."

"Then why are you managing it?"

The big lobster thought about it seriously. "Because the treasure looked like it really needed managing."

Following its gaze, Pangpang realized there was a stone door hidden deep inside the cavern. The big lobster walked up and knocked on the stone door three times with its claw. The door slowly groaned open, revealing piles of diamonds, gold ingots, iron ingots, and a whole chest of sparkling End Ores.

Pangpang immediately began calculating how many sets of armor all this could craft.

"Where did this treasure come from?" Steve asked.

The Ender Lobster replied, "I mined some of it, people gave me some of it, and the system rewarded me with the rest after I sorted the stones into round ones and not-so-round ones."

"You learned how to do that after drinking coffee?"

"I drank three cups," the big lobster said. "And that was the only thing I learned."

Pangpang pointed at the empty coffee cup. "That cup of coffee was mine."

The big lobster quickly raised both claws. "Don't worry! I can compensate you with treasure."

Pangpang instantly stood up straight. "I want the biggest chest."

Steve tugged at his sleeve. "He just said he'd compensate us. He didn't say you could pick whatever you want."

The big lobster paced around the treasure room before pointing its claw at a corner. "There are three chests there. You can't have the biggest one, because that holds my winter plans. You can't have the middle one, because that's the treasure registry. You can pick the smallest one."

Pangpang's expression slowly turned serious.

"The smallest chests usually contain the most abnormal things."

Steve said, "You literally just made that up right now."

"Things you make up can also be true."

As soon as they walked over to the small chest, a sharp *hiss* came from the pile of treasure.

A Creeper wearing a golden helmet stepped out from between the diamonds and iron ingots. It was holding up a sign that read:

"Treasure Administrator. Loud cheering prohibited."

Pangpang's smile vanished.

Steve slowly backed away.

The little Ender Dragon gasped.

The Ender Lobster hurriedly said, "Wait, this is the temporary administrator. He's a bit strict."

The Creeper Administrator flipped the sign over.

The back read:

"Quiet cheering also prohibited."

Pangpang lowered his voice to a whisper. "We're just here to get compensation for a cup of coffee."

The Creeper Administrator nodded. "Permitted."

Pangpang pointed at the smallest chest. "What about this one?"

"Opening is permitted."

"And the stuff inside?"

"Looking is permitted."

"Can we take it?"

The Creeper Administrator pushed the sign forward slightly.

"Open it first, then we'll talk."

Pangpang decided to employ what he considered a foolproof method. He pulled the End-Alloy Coffee Portafilter from his pocket and gently sprinkled the 3.618 grams of tin onto the floor, forming an imperfect circle.

The Creeper Administrator stared at the tin circle for a long while.

"What is this?"

Pangpang kept a straight face. "An advanced mineshaft silencing ritual."

"What does it do?"

"It keeps everyone quiet for three seconds."

The Creeper Administrator clearly found the ritual suspicious, but it looked down at its sign anyway, as if checking its own rules to see if silencing rituals were prohibited.

The first second, no one spoke.

The second second, a soft clatter echoed from the minecart tracks in the distance.

The third second, the little Ender Dragon sneezed.

"Awoo-choo!"

Purple flames shot out of its nose and landed right on the Creeper Administrator's golden helmet. The helmet didn't explode; instead, a delicious aroma filled the air, and its surface rapidly transformed into golden popcorn.

The Creeper Administrator touched its head.

At first, it froze, but then it started laughing.

"So I was wearing a popcorn helmet!"

It hopped happily in place, the ground trembling slightly beneath its feet. But suddenly, it stopped abruptly and flipped the sign to the front.

"Wait, no," it said. "Loud cheering prohibited."

The Ender Lobster covered its mouth with a claw, but couldn't help letting out a soft *click*.

The Creeper Administrator looked at everyone, and finally handed the key to Pangpang.

"You may open one chest. You cannot choose the biggest one."

Pangpang immediately shoved the key into the smallest chest.

The sound of the key turning echoed through the cavern.

Inside the chest, there were no diamonds, no gold ingots, and no new coffee.

There was only a piece of paper.

"Congratulations on unlocking the next clue: go find the talking minecart."

Pangpang held the paper up to his face. "I knew it."

Steve asked, "Knew what?"

"The smallest chest is the most abnormal. And abnormal things usually lead us to even bigger trouble."

Steve sighed. "You're not being calm. You're just getting used to it."

## After the Three-Second Silencing Ritual, the Minecart Woke Up

The Ender Lobster led the way.

It used its giant claws to carve marks into the walls. Pangpang was in charge of reading the map, Steve was in charge of checking for traps underfoot, and the little Ender Dragon was in charge of teleporting anyone who fell behind to the very front.

The division of labor sounded perfectly reasonable, but executing it was quite chaotic.

The moment Pangpang drew an X on the map, the little Ender Dragon teleported him dead center onto the X. The moment Steve discovered a pressure plate, the Ender Lobster had already picked it up with its claws. The Creeper Administrator carried its "No Cheering" sign the whole way, only to trip over a low-hanging vine and nearly trigger a nearby tripwire.

Fortunately, the minecart tracks stretched continuously deep into the mineshaft.

They followed the tracks for a long time, eventually hearing a *clack-clack-clack* sound ahead. The sound drew closer, then faded, as if a minecart were driving in circles in the dark.

Finally, a minecart shot out from around the corner.

Sitting inside the cart was a sheep wearing glasses.

The minecart stopped in front of Pangpang, and the sheep pushed its glasses up. "Get in. Next stop: Lost Canyon."

Pangpang was stunned. "You're the talking minecart?"

The sheep said, "No, I'm a passenger."

Steve looked down into the cart. "Where's the minecart, then?"

The sheep pointed a hoof at the bottom of the cart.

Inside sat a pillow, and the cart's wheels were emitting soft snoring sounds. It turned out the minecart was asleep, and had merely coasted down the incline of the tracks.

The little Ender Dragon found the sound amusing and flew over to pat the edge of the cart.

The minecart jolted awake.

"Who stepped on my wheels?!" it yelled. "I haven't even clocked out yet!"

Pangpang hurriedly explained that they had come looking for it based on the note in the small chest. After listening, the minecart looked at Pangpang, then at the little Ender Dragon, and finally at the crooked diamond helmet on the Ender Lobster's head.

"This group of yours doesn't look like they're here for a ride."

"What do we look like we're here for?" Steve asked.

"Like you're here to force my tracks into early retirement."

Pangpang immediately promised, "We won't break the tracks."

The minecart said, "The last people who said that ended up sending me into the Creeper's treasure room."

The Creeper Administrator raised its sign: "Hitchhiking in the treasure room is prohibited."

"See?" the minecart said. "Your own acquaintances have proved it."

Steve pulled an iron ingot from his inventory. "We can buy tickets."

The minecart's wheels immediately went quiet.

"One iron ingot, or one cup of coffee."

Pangpang clutched the coffee portafilter tightly to his chest. "Little End drank the coffee."

The minecart sighed. "Iron ingot it is, then. Prices in the mineshaft have gone up lately; even the Creeper's helmet was downgraded to popcorn."

Steve dropped the iron ingot into the cart.

The sheep scooted to the side to make room for everyone.

Pangpang, Steve, the little Ender Dragon, the Ender Lobster, and the Creeper Administrator all squeezed into the same minecart. Pangpang sat at the very front, gripping the edges, repeatedly reminding everyone not to stick their feet out.

The little Ender Dragon perched on his head.

The minecart cleared its throat. "Next stop: Lost Canyon. Non-stop service, no refunds, and we hold no liability for passengers teleported out of the cart by their own pets."

Pangpang suddenly felt like they had bought their tickets a little too hastily.

When the minecart first started moving, it wasn't very fast.

The wheels rolled over the tracks, producing a rhythmic clatter. The torches on the cavern walls blurred into a streak of yellow dots behind them. The sheep wearing glasses closed its eyes, looking as though it had taken this route many times before.

Pangpang finally relaxed.

He asked the sheep, "What's in Lost Canyon?"

The sheep replied, "Roads."

"What else?"

"More roads."

"Then how will we know which one is the right one?"

The sheep opened one eye. "You don't. That's why it's called Lost Canyon."

The Ender Lobster found this answer incredibly profound, rested its claws on the edge of the cart, and began telling everyone the stories of getting lost it had accumulated over three hundred years. Halfway through the first story, the minecart began to accelerate; before the second story could finish, the minecart had blasted through two forks in the tracks; and by the time it reached the conclusion of the third story, the minecart suddenly let out a long scream.

"Construction on the tracks ahead!"

Pangpang looked forward.

A sign stood up ahead:

"Sprinting Prohibited."

Instead of slowing down, the minecart actually sped up.

Pangpang gripped the edge of the cart tighter. "Why does it always drive toward the things that are prohibited?"

The minecart answered proudly, "Because I am the fastest driver in the mineshaft!"

Steve looked ahead. "Fast doesn't mean safe."

"Safety compromises speed."

The little Ender Dragon excitedly spread its wings, purple flames licking out from the tip of its tail. Pangpang quickly pressed its wings down. "No accelerating on the ride."

Taking that as a challenge, the minecart's wheels spun even faster.

The glow of lava ahead grew brighter.

## The Tracks Broke Above the Lava

As they shot past the final curve, the mineshaft suddenly opened up.

Below them lay a massive river of lava.

Lava cascaded from a high plateau on the left, spreading out along the black stone walls into the distance. Heat rose from the surface, baking the distant air red. Suspended above the lava river was a railway bridge, leading to a canyon shrouded in purple mist on the other side.

The minecart was hurtling across the bridge.

Pangpang saw a gap in the tracks ahead.

The gap was only four blocks wide, but it sat perfectly on the narrowest section of the bridge. The broken rails dangled in mid-air, and the surrounding blocks had already been scorched black by the lava.

"Brake!" Pangpang yelled.

The minecart said, "I don't have brakes."

"Then stop!"

"Stopping compromises speed."

Steve stood up, grabbing the front lip of the cart, attempting to lift the wheels off the tracks. The Ender Lobster extended both claws, trying to clamp the cart shut. The Creeper Administrator turned its "No Cheering" sign sideways to use as an improvised shield.

The sheep wearing glasses finally opened its eyes.

"I forgot to tell you," it said. "This bridge isn't finished today."

Pangpang looked back. "Why are you only saying this now?"

"Because there was no need to say it until now."

The little Ender Dragon fluttered up from Pangpang's head and spat a tiny puff of purple fire toward the broken tracks. The fire didn't reconnect the rails, but it did scorch a nearby block of stone until it glowed.

The minecart reached the gap.

The moment the wheels left the tracks, everything went dead silent.

Pangpang watched his backpack slide off his shoulder, the coffee portafilter clinking softly inside. Steve reached out to grab the edge of the cart, the Ender Lobster dug a claw into the stone wall, the Creeper Administrator held up its sign defensively, and the sheep adjusted its glasses.

The little Ender Dragon turned its head to look at them in mid-air.

It seemed to finally understand that it had led everyone to a very bad place for teleportation practice.

Pangpang stretched his hand out. "Little End, don't teleport indiscriminately."

The little Ender Dragon hovered in the air.

"Awoo."

It opened its mouth, and a ball of purple light gathered in its throat.

Pangpang's heart sank. "I said no random teleporting, and no random fire breathing!"

But the flame had already launched.

It didn't hit the broken tracks, nor did it hit the minecart. Instead, it landed on a black block on the far side of the lava river. Suddenly, the block emitted a pulse of purple light, as if an invisible door had just been opened.

The Ender Lobster saw the light first.

"That's not lava," it said. "That's an old teleportation waypoint for the End Mineshaft."

Steve stared at the waypoint. "Can you send the minecart through it?"

The minecart immediately protested, "I don't ride portals without tracks."

Watching the front of the cart dip lower and lower, Pangpang quickly checked his inventory. He had three blocks of cobblestone, one water bucket, half a stack of wooden planks, a nearly broken iron pickaxe, and the glowing coffee portafilter roasted by the little Ender Dragon.

"I can patch a section of track," he said.

Steve asked, "Do you have rails?"

Pangpang fell silent.

The Creeper Administrator flipped its sign to the back.

"Altering mineshaft structure outside the treasure room is temporarily prohibited."

"We're not in the treasure room right now," Pangpang said.

"Then it's temporarily not prohibited."

The Ender Lobster yanked its claw out of the stone wall, leaning its body forward in an attempt to clamp onto the back half of the minecart. Its shell baked under the intense heat, and the popcorn helmet on its head began to pop off, piece by piece.

The sheep wearing glasses pulled an old map out of the cart.

"There's a backup track here," it said.

Pangpang looked at the map. "Where?"

The sheep pointed its hoof across the lava river.

Steve asked, "How do we get across?"

The sheep replied, "No idea."

"Then why did you say there's a backup track?"

"Because it's drawn on the map."

The minecart's front wheels had already breached the edge of the gap.

Pangpang closed his eyes. He thought about the half-finished safe house from his very first Ender Dragon fight, the hundreds of times he ran all the way from spawn back to the End, and the dragon egg that just wouldn't stay put on its display pedestal.

He suddenly realized that the thing he was best at wasn't fighting dragons or mining.

His greatest skill was continuing to find a solution that seemed "not quite too late" even when it was clearly past the point of no return.

Pangpang handed the three blocks of cobblestone to Steve. "Build a platform."

He passed the wooden planks to the Ender Lobster. "Can you wedge the cart?"

He handed the coffee portafilter to the little Ender Dragon. "Don't drink it, and don't teleport it. Just breathe the smallest puff of fire at that glowing black block over there."

The little Ender Dragon looked down at the portafilter, then looked across the lava river.

It didn't act immediately.

The distant waypoint hummed with a low vibration, sounding as though something massive were waking up deep inside the mineshaft.

The minecart dangled over the broken tracks, lava churning violently below them.

Gripping the edge of the cart, Pangpang yelled, "Little End, teleport the coffee across first!"

The little Ender Dragon spread its wings and soared toward the far side of the lava river.

Behind it, the minecart tumbled right off the broken tracks.

## Another Path Lay Hidden Beneath the Lava

The minecart plummeted faster than Pangpang had expected.

He originally thought there would be enough falling time for Steve to build a platform out of cobblestone, or for the little Ender Dragon to pull off a precise teleportation. But the minecart only managed half a flip in mid-air before its wheels crashed into a protruding block of obsidian below.

*Crash!*

The cart bounced sideways.

Pangpang was thrown into Steve, who crashed into the Ender Lobster. The Ender Lobster's claws bent the Creeper Administrator's sign into a sharp right angle. The sheep wearing glasses clung tightly to the pillow in the cart, which had the words "Do Not Break" scrawled on it.

Looking back mid-air, the little Ender Dragon saw everyone flying out of the cart.

Instead of continuing toward the waypoint, it teleported back to the minecart.

Purple light burst outward from the little dragon like a net that hadn't fully unfurled. Pangpang's backpack snagged on the light, Steve's scabbard hooked onto it, and the Ender Lobster's tail got tangled up with the Creeper Administrator's sign.

The minecart wasn't caught.

It plunged straight into the lava.

The wheels hit the magma first, followed by the body of the cart. The minecart let out a highly dissatisfied shriek: "I'm not clocked out for the day!"

In the next second, the surface of the lava was split open by a beam of purple light.

Pangpang, Steve, the Ender Lobster, the Creeper Administrator, the sheep wearing glasses, and the little Ender Dragon all tumbled into the fissure.

They weren't baked into block-shaped bread.

Beneath the lava, there was actually a stone corridor sloping downward, its walls lined with glowing End Ores. The minecart was wedged in the center of the corridor; its chassis hadn't melted, but the iron on its wheels glowed red-hot.

The minecart remained silent for a moment.

"This is not on my route."

Pangpang scrambled up from the floor. "Didn't you say you were the fastest driver in the mineshaft?"

"I didn't say I was the best driver at reading maps."

The Ender Lobster used its claws to push aside the obstructing rocks, revealing a much deeper, purple-hued space at the end of the corridor. Abandoned tracks lay scattered about, holding minecarts of various designs. Some were overgrown with vines, others had wheels encased in crystals, and one minecart even had a sign hanging from it:

"To the Depths of the End. Passengers ride at their own risk."

Seeing the sign, the sheep wearing glasses immediately took off its glasses and wiped them.

"This is the backup track."

Steve asked, "You've been here before?"

The sheep shook its head. "No."

Pangpang looked at the little Ender Dragon.

The little Ender Dragon was perched on the front of their minecart, placing Pangpang's coffee portafilter right next to its claws. The purple glow from the portafilter trickled along the abandoned tracks, eventually gathering into a tiny spark of light on a door at the far end.

From behind that door came a familiar sound.

It sounded like the footsteps of an Enderman, or perhaps a dragon egg wobbling gently in a chest.

Pangpang picked up the note he had dropped earlier. Originally, it only read "go find the talking minecart." Now, a new line of text had appeared on the back:

"Once you find the minecart, don't let it pick the route."

Upon seeing the note, the minecart's wheels immediately fell silent.

"Who wrote that?"

The Ender Lobster slowly raised a claw. "Probably the same person who sent you into the treasure room three hundred years ago."

The Creeper Administrator straightened its bent sign. "Or maybe it was me."

"You know how to write?" Pangpang asked.

The Creeper Administrator looked down at its sign. "I only know how to write 'prohibited'."

A low rumble echoed from deep within the corridor.

The little Ender Dragon lifted its head and flew toward the door.

Pangpang quickly yelled, "Little End, wait!"

The little Ender Dragon stopped in front of the door and looked back at him.

This time, it didn't immediately teleport anything away, nor did it send the coffee portafilter far away. It simply reached out and gently nudged the purple spark on the door with its claw.

The door cracked open.

Through the crack, a railway track leading upward was revealed.

At the end of the track, a breeze seemed to be blowing down.

Pangpang hoisted his iron pickaxe back onto his shoulder. Steve checked his sword edge. The Ender Lobster adjusted its crooked diamond helmet. The Creeper Administrator put its sign away, and the sheep wearing glasses stuffed its pillow back into the minecart.

The minecart asked, "Are you guys still riding?"

Pangpang looked at the upward track. "Who's picking the route this time?"

The little Ender Dragon flew back to his side and earnestly picked up the coffee portafilter in its mouth.

Pangpang's expression changed. "I didn't tell you to drink it."

The little Ender Dragon didn't drink it.

It spat a tiny puff of purple flame toward the end of the track.

A response echoed from the distance—sounding somewhat like a dragon, somewhat like a minecart, and somewhat like something entirely more massive.

The minecart's wheels slowly began to spin.

"Next stop," it announced. "The end of Lost Canyon."

Pangpang grabbed the edge of the cart. "Take it slower this time."

The minecart replied, "I'll try."

Steve asked, "How slow is 'try'?"

The minecart didn't answer.

Because it had already blasted forward.

The little Ender Dragon flew at the very front, its purple fire illuminating the ascending tracks. Pangpang clung tightly to the coffee portafilter. The Ender Lobster gripped the cart with its claws, the Creeper Administrator held its sign to its chest like a shield, the sheep wearing glasses squeezed its eyes shut, and over the roar of the wheels, Steve counted how many curves lay ahead.

They didn't know where the tracks would lead.

Nor did they know exactly who had left that note behind the door.

But they knew one thing: as long as the little Ender Dragon kept teleporting coffee to weird places, and as long as the minecart kept sprinting where it shouldn't sprint, Pangpang would always find the next block to step on.

As for whether they'd fall into lava again next time? They could discuss that once the minecart managed to brake.

If it was willing to brake, that is.

And the tracks stretching upward are taking them even deeper into the mineshaft.
