Steve and Pangpang set out for the first time before dawn.

A rainstorm had just washed through the valley, leaving water droplets on the grass. In the distance, the lava pool looked like a piece of red glass that hadn't been covered properly. Pangpang clutched half a loaf of bread and wore a backpack that clearly couldn't hold much, stopping every three steps to make sure his bread was still there.

Steve rested his diamond pickaxe on his shoulder and asked, "Are you here to mine obsidian, or to protect that half a loaf of bread?"

"Both are important," Pangpang said. "Without obsidian, we can't enter the Nether; without bread, I'll have a hard time even walking to the lava pool."

The goal of their adventure was clear: mine enough obsidian, build a portal, make their way to the End, and finally find the Ender Dragon. Pangpang had already figured out the ending before they left—he would stand at the front of the team, Steve would follow up with the final blows, and Alex would direct from the side. As for whether he would be killed by the Ender Dragon, Pangpang considered that a "detail that didn't need to be discussed in advance."

Later, facts proved that this detail really didn't need to be discussed in advance, because they died many times before they even reached the End.

## The Overtime Simulator Next to the Respawn Point

There was no obsidian ore right away by the lava pool. The two of them first ran into Endermen on a low patch of black stone ground.

The Enderman stood in the fog, its body as tall as a misplaced pillar, its purple eyes watching them quietly. Pangpang whispered, "Let's go around it."

Steve also whispered, "Okay."

Then, out of nervousness, Pangpang looked up.

The Enderman vanished.

The next second, Pangpang was sent flying. The half loaf of bread spun twice in the air and landed at Steve's feet. Steve picked up the bread, turned around, and saw that Pangpang had already collapsed on the black stone ground.

They respawned and ran all the way back. The second time, Pangpang said he had mastered the pattern—just don't look the Enderman in the eyes. The third time, he claimed he had only glanced at the reflection on the ground. The fourth time, he insisted it wasn't looking, but "confirming the terrain." By the sixth time, he began to suspect that the ground was intentionally reflecting the Enderman's eyes at him.

Eventually, they lost count of how many times they had died. They only knew that the ceiling at the respawn point always looked the same, and the chest by the bed was always in the same spot. Pangpang called this black wasteland the "Overtime Simulator," while Steve carved a number on a block of dirt. He stopped carving after it reached 100 because he noticed Pangpang would recalculate his death count every time he saw the number.

Slowly, they learned not to get greedy with their attacks. Hit once, step back twice; hide first when seeing purple particles, don't chase; build a temporary shelter if there isn't one. Pangpang took the front line, and Steve looked for openings. Every time Pangpang charged out, he would say, "I've got this one," and then get teleported in another direction in less than three seconds.

During their most dangerous encounter, Pangpang dug out the Totem of Undying from the bottom of his backpack, which he had been reluctant to use.

"Is this what you called the 'Resurrection Chicken'?" Steve asked.

"It doesn't look like a chicken, but it's responsible for resurrecting," Pangpang held up the totem. "Today, we call it the Resurrection Chicken."

He rushed forward and tanked a round of attacks from the Enderman for Steve. The totem shattered into a burst of golden light in his hand, pulling him back from the brink of death. His face was paler than before, yet he insisted, "See that? I didn't even die just now."

Steve didn't argue with him. He waited for the brief pause after the Enderman teleported, stepped sideways, swung his sword, and retreated. Pangpang absorbed the frontline damage, and Steve seized the openings time and time again, finally disrupting the rhythm of the Endermen. They didn't win beautifully; they just held on a little longer than before, and then a little longer, until they finally escaped the black wasteland.

![Steve and Pangpang surrounded by a circle of Endermen. Beds emitting purple particles are repeatedly placed around them. Pangpang hides behind a dirt shelter while Steve raises his pickaxe, ready to pull him out.](/uploads/2026/08/16/Minecraft_长篇故事博客稿/02.png)

They found diamonds in a branch of the cave.

Not just one or two pieces, but an entire wall of blue light.

Pangpang was quiet for a few seconds, then raised his pickaxe above his head: "It's not the Overtime Simulator today; today it's the Year-End Bonus Simulator."

They mined everything around the ore vein to make sure they didn't miss a single piece. Steve was in charge of watching the lava, and Pangpang was in charge of mining. By the end, both of their backpacks were so heavy they could barely walk. Pangpang counted every diamond twice. Halfway through the second count, he forgot what the first count was, so he had to start all over again.

The sun had already risen by the time they walked out of the cave. Looking at the diamond pickaxe on his shoulder, Steve felt that this day had finally paid off. Pangpang, on the other hand, felt he would never have to worry about gear again, and even started discussing whether they could use diamonds to pave the campsite floor.

## Alex's Three Team Rules

Someone was already waiting for them at the campsite.

Alex sat by the chest, holding a map in her hand. She looked first at Steve, then at Pangpang's backpack, which was bulging as if it contained a sheep, and finally asked, "Do you two have something you haven't told me?"

Pangpang tried to hide the backpack behind him, but the movement was so jerky that a few diamonds rolled out of the opening.

Alex didn't get angry immediately. She picked up the diamonds, placed them on the table, and said, "From today on, I'm joining your journey."

Steve asked, "Are you here to adventure together?"

"And to manage you two."

Alex's division of labor was quickly settled. Steve was in charge of scouting and mining; Pangpang was in charge of carrying things and taking on tasks that looked brave but were actually dangerous; Alex was in charge of routes, supplies, and retreats. She also wrote down three team rules: report any resources found first, all items are centrally distributed, and whoever runs off on their own is responsible for the night watch.

These three rules sounded simple, but Steve and Pangpang quickly proved that even simple rules could be repeatedly broken by them.

The first time they secretly mined emeralds behind Alex's back, Pangpang stuffed his backpack so full that emeralds rolled all the way from the opening to the cave entrance. Following the green glow, Alex found the two of them squatting by the cave wall, each holding a pickaxe.

Steve said, "These grew out of the ground themselves."

Pangpang added, "And they grew very suddenly."

Alex pulled out her bow and shot an arrow at each of them.

The arrows didn't deal real damage, but they sent them back to the respawn point.

Pangpang sat up from the bed, and his first words were, "Isn't your management style a bit too direct?"

Steve said, "At least she didn't confiscate the bread."

After returning to the campsite, they scolded Alex, arguing that she shouldn't use a bow to solve team communication problems. After listening, Alex divided the emeralds into three portions and told them that in the future, they must shout out first when they find resources, and then decide how to distribute them. Pangpang suggested "covering your own eyes before getting rich," and Steve recommended sewing bigger pockets on the backpacks. Alex adopted neither suggestion.

Later, they hid lapis lazuli and redstone again.

This time there were no arrows. There was only a table in the camp, a quill, and a stack of paper. The top of the paper read: "A Profound Self-Reflection on Why I Shouldn't Hide Resources a Second Time." Pangpang stared at the line for a long time, saying that writing reflections was more tiring than mining. Steve picked up the pen, saying he was willing to take primary responsibility, but by the third line, he changed "I shouldn't hide resources" to "I shouldn't lack transparency in resource management."

Alex took one look and flipped the paper over.

Even later, they discovered more minerals. The three of them agreed to mine together, with no one reporting. Pangpang and Steve high-fived, declaring this operation absolutely confidential. Steve had barely taken two steps out of the cave when he suddenly turned and sprinted toward the camp.

"Report!" he yelled as he ran. "I voluntarily confess, asking for leniency!"

Standing in front of the cave wall, Pangpang felt his brotherhood was like a wooden plank placed next to lava—it was already starting to smoke.

Alex didn't send them back to the respawn point, nor did she fight them. She simply confiscated all the minerals and made both of them write a self-reflection. Pangpang wrote "Why I Shouldn't Package Delayed Reporting as Management Science," and Steve wrote "Why I Shouldn't High-Five to Form an Alliance and Then Voluntarily Snitch."

Pangpang didn't intend to forgive Steve, until Steve actually chased after him.

Steve didn't explain why he ran, nor did he shift the blame to Alex. He just said, "I was afraid of being sent back to the respawn point again, so I ran first. I was wrong."

Pangpang said, "You sure ran fast."

But his hand broke off half of his bread and handed it to Steve.

The two of them had made peace.

They later mined a massive pile of obsidian behind a cave wall, only to find the total was an odd number. No one could claim the extra block. Steve placed it in the middle of the three of them and said, "Let's leave it here for now."

Pangpang asked, "Until when?"

Alex said, "Until you figure out whether things are meant to divide people, or to keep people moving forward together."

## Who Gets the Enchanted Book

By the time they found a blacksmith in a village, the three of them had learned to temporarily stop fighting over things.

This progress lasted for less than a minute.

In the blacksmith's chest, there was an enchanted book with Sharpness V, Unbreaking III, and Mending all on its pages. Pangpang reached for it, and Steve reached for it too. Their hands collided in mid-air. Alex was one step faster; she picked up the book and placed it on the anvil.

"Who gets it?" Pangpang asked.

Steve said, "My sword needs it more."

Pangpang said, "My mace needs it too."

Alex said, "Put it in the middle for now."

![In the village blacksmith, a powerful enchanted book hovers above the anvil. Steve and Pangpang reach for it at the same time, while Alex points to a chest labeled 'Put it in the middle for now'.](/uploads/2026/08/16/Minecraft_长篇故事博客稿/05.png)

<small>Figure 05 | One enchanted book, three people who all feel they need it more</small>

She found a chest in the blacksmith's shop, placed the enchanted book inside, and stuck a small sign on the chest. The sign read: "Important items. Discuss before use."

Pangpang looked at the sign and felt the team was transforming from an adventure squad into a heavily regulated warehouse.

During the few days they stayed near the village, they continued looking for new caves. One day, Steve discovered a cavity behind a wall blocked by stones. The three of them dug it open together, revealing a deeply hidden secret chamber.

Inside the chamber sat exactly three full sets of enchanted diamond armor.

Each set was identical.

Pangpang asked first, "How do we split this time?"

Alex looked at the three sets of armor, then at the three of them, and said, "One set each."

Pangpang stood frozen in place. He was probably so used to every item sparking a conflict that when faced with gear perfectly divided for everyone, he suddenly didn't know how to start arguing.

Steve put on the set on the left, Alex put on the set on the right, and Pangpang slid the middle set on from head to toe. He took two steps and nearly tripped himself because the armor was too heavy.

"At least we don't have to fight over it this time," Alex said.

"But that makes the story less funny," Pangpang said.

![Three identical sets of enchanted diamond armor are neatly displayed in three stone niches of a secret chamber. Steve, Pangpang, and Alex reach out at the same time, with no reason to fight anymore.](/uploads/2026/08/16/Minecraft_长篇故事博客稿/06.png)

<small>Figure 06 | Exactly three sets, finally no need to fight</small>

## Stepping into Traps Wearing Year-End Bonuses

After putting on the diamond armor, Pangpang's walking posture became noticeably arrogant.

They soon found a temple in the jungle. Alex stood at the entrance observing for ten seconds, confirming there were no arrow slits on the left and no obvious pressure plates on the right. Steve said it was safe to enter, and Pangpang claimed that since he was wearing a full set of enchanted diamond armor, even if a trap was triggered, it would only tickle him.

Just as he finished speaking, the floor under his feet clicked.

The first volley of arrows shot out of the wall, and all three raised their shields simultaneously. Pangpang's heel stepped on a second pressure plate, causing the ceiling to start lowering. Steve reached out to pull him, while Alex found a hidden lever within the wall's patterns. She pulled it down hard, and the stone door opened at the last possible moment.

As they dashed out of the hall, all three sets of diamond armor were flashing low durability warnings.

Pangpang looked down at his armor: "I'm retiring right after taking the job."

Steve said, "We came to step on landmines wearing our year-end bonuses."

Alex didn't reply to them. She walked back to the temple entrance, re-examined the patterns on the walls, and finally found a trapdoor beneath a piece of rubble.

Beneath the trapdoor wasn't another round of traps, but two enormous chests.

When the chests were opened, the entire hidden room was bathed in purple enchanted light. Inside were piles of brand-new diamond armor—helmets, chestplates, leggings, and boots, all neatly arranged, as if someone had anticipated they would ruin their newly acquired gear by stepping on traps.

Pangpang took out a helmet and asked, "Is this compensation?"

Alex said, "These are spares."

Pangpang pulled out another one: "What about this one?"

"Also a spare."

He looked back at the piles of armor and said, "So, can we divide the spares into spares for the spares?"

No one answered him. Alex had already started taking inventory of the chests.

## The Shadow Dragon Outside the Temple

As they closed the chests again, the area outside the temple suddenly fell silent.

The chirping of bugs vanished, the wind stopped, and even the leaves seemed to be waiting for something. Steve walked to the door and saw a shadow darker than the night sky. The shadow spread its wings, revealing purple eyes. Its massive tail swept across the temple roof, sending a few rocks tumbling down.

It was a Shadow Dragon.

Pangpang raised his shield, declaring he would draw its attention. The next second, the dragon's tail brushed past his backpack, sending him tumbling down the stairs. As he stood up, he was still saying, "I was proactively adjusting my position."

Alex didn't let them take it head-on. She pointed to a broken pillar outside the temple, instructing Steve to find an angle from a high vantage point, while she took charge of luring the dragon out to the open area. Pangpang was to hold the stairs, preventing the dragon from charging into the hidden chamber.

The battle lasted a long time. The three constantly shifted positions, their armor repeatedly making sounds as if it were about to shatter. The Shadow Dragon crashed into a stone pillar, briefly trapping its body in the rubble. Steve and Alex struck simultaneously, and Pangpang charged out from behind his shield. The attacks from all three landed at almost the exact same moment.

The Shadow Dragon fell.

They didn't cheer immediately. Beneath the dragon's body was a black egg covered in purple veins, looking like a stone that was slowly breathing.

Pangpang asked, "Is this edible?"

Alex raised a hand to press down on his head: "Stay away from it."

They built a small shelter next to the temple and placed the dragon egg near the fire. Deep in the night, the eggshell began to vibrate, and cracks slowly covered its surface. Pangpang wanted to hand over a piece of bread, but Alex blocked his hand preemptively.

The shell shattered, and a tiny black-and-purple flying dragon wriggled out. It first looked at the bread in Pangpang's hand, then at Alex's bow, and finally turned and walked over to Steve's boots, gently nudging his instep with its head.

Steve squatted down and reached out his hand.

The Little Shadow Dragon rested its head in his palm.

Pangpang quickly announced that he was the little dragon's distant relative, only for the dragon to sneeze and toast the bread in his hand black. Pangpang immediately corrected himself, saying he was just a casual acquaintance.

He once tried to give the little dragon a shorter name, but Alex said there were already enough titles in the story, and they shouldn't just haphazardly name pets. So they kept calling it the Little Shadow Dragon, or sometimes just the Little Dragon.

## The Wither Needs an Owner Too

After the Little Shadow Dragon joined the team, Pangpang felt the pet distribution had become unbalanced.

Steve had a dragon, Alex didn't, and neither did Pangpang. Pangpang deemed this a severe management issue that needed to be rectified immediately. Alex argued that pets weren't issued per person. Pangpang retorted they should at least be issued based on contributions. When Steve asked what exactly he had contributed lately, Pangpang said he had contributed a lot of experience in getting sent flying.

They encountered the Wither in an abandoned fortress in the snowy mountains.

When the summoning core shattered, a black mist rose from the ground, and its three heads turned toward them simultaneously. Steve took on drawing its fire, while Alex constantly shifted her position. Pangpang was blasted away three times, and every time he landed, he yelled, "I held it off!"

The Little Shadow Dragon shattered the final sealing stone. The Wither's body swayed in the air and then violently dispersed. Once the black mist cleared, a Mini Wither the size of a wolf was left in the snow. It didn't resume its attack but slowly floated up to Alex, gently nudging her glove with its middle head.

Pangpang remained silent for a long time.

"Steve has a dragon, you have a Wither," he pointed at the two of them. "What about me?"

A mimicking squawk came from a corner of the fortress. A red-and-blue parrot flew out, landed on Pangpang's head, and yelled in a voice that sounded just like an enemy commander: "My armor is expensive!"

Steve laughed so hard he couldn't stand straight. Alex couldn't help but laugh either. Pangpang took the parrot off his head and asked if it could be a little quieter. The parrot replied: "My armor is expensive!"

Pangpang finally accepted it.

He still felt his pet talked too much, but the parrot flew onto his head on its own, which was something he couldn't refuse.

## Over Two Hundred Players and a Chokepoint

After the three of them left the snow mountain with their three pets, they encountered over two hundred players.

The opponents wanted the enchanted diamond armor they had just found, and they also wanted the pets by their sides. The moment Pangpang heard this, he immediately hid the parrot behind him. The parrot poked its head over his shoulder and mimicked the opponent's commander: "Everyone retreat!"

The other side genuinely fell into a brief chaos.

Alex seized this opening and quickly surveyed the terrain. Ahead was a very narrow stone slope, bordered by high walls on both the left and right. As long as they held the entrance, the enemy's numerical advantage would be hard to deploy simultaneously. She assigned Steve and the Little Shadow Dragon to hold the left side, herself and the Mini Wither to the middle, while Pangpang would charge down from high ground with his shield. The parrot was responsible for continuing its vocal mimicry.

Steve didn't try to single-handedly defeat everyone. He only swung his sword when opponents drew close, then immediately retreated behind the stone pillars. The Little Shadow Dragon spewed flames, blocking off a flank route. The Mini Wither kept the aerial formations from getting too close. Pangpang charged swiftly, but this time, he knew to stop before losing his balance.

In the end, the two hundred-plus players weren't entirely wiped out by them. Instead, they lost their judgment due to this incomprehensible coordination. Thinking there were more people behind the chokepoint, they slowly backed away and finally gave up the robbery.

Pangpang watched the distant crowd and asked, "Did we win?"

Alex said, "We held the line."

The two phrases sounded similar, but they all knew that holding the line was more important than chasing after them to win.

![Steve, Alex, and Pangpang stand high on a chokepoint with their three pets. In the distance is a crowd of players made of black blocks, while the parrot creates chaos in the air.](/uploads/2026/08/16/Minecraft_长篇故事博客稿/12.png)

While they were traveling, Pangpang slipped near a volcano.

His foot stepped on a loose rock, and his body slid downward. Steve lunged forward to grab his hand, dragging himself closer to the edge. Alex lay flat on the ground and yanked Steve's ankles.

The Little Shadow Dragon bit onto Steve's backpack strap, flapping its wings desperately. The Mini Wither hovered in mid-air, pushing back against the direction of the fall. The parrot flew to the opposite bank and squawked at a mountain goat on the slope. The goat lowered its head and rammed a loose stone down, which got wedged perfectly in the middle of the slope.

Using the support of that stone, they slowly pulled Pangpang back up.

Pangpang lay panting on the ground, and his first words were: "I thought no one was going to pull me."

Steve yanked him up from the ground: "Don't carry it all by yourself."

Alex added: "Shout ahead of time next time."

Pangpang nodded. After a while, he asked, "Does that mountain goat just now count as a temporary team member?"

No one answered. The parrot answered for them: "My armor is expensive!"

## A Tide of Thousands

What truly forced them apart was an endless siege of overwhelming numbers.

Thousands of players pushed in from both sides of the snowfield. Explosions, arrows, and smoke chopped the sky into pieces. The three had used every trick available, but they still couldn't hold back the crowd. Alex was forced to retreat east, Steve flew north with the Little Shadow Dragon, and Pangpang was blown behind a snowy slope by a shockwave.

For the first time, they didn't wake up at the same respawn point.

Steve was found by a small squad of kind-hearted players. They didn't ask where his gear was; they just gave him bread and potions and told him to survive first. They taught Steve how to deploy his elytra, how to change direction using firework rockets, and how to read the terrain before landing.

Pangpang was rescued by another group of players. This group talked a lot. First, they outfitted him in new gear, then spent a long time teaching him how to use the mace and wind charges. They emphasized one thing repeatedly: the mace wasn't meant for charging forward blindly; stand firm first, then strike.

Alex encountered a very quiet veteran player. They didn't lecture her on techniques; they merely took her to observe a forest, teaching her when to rescue someone, when to retreat, and when to acknowledge that a battle was not worth continuing.

The three of them learned in different camps.

The first time Steve flew over a ridge using a firework rocket, he almost crashed into a tree. The first time Pangpang swung his mace, he bounced himself into the water. The first time Alex decided to retreat, the people behind her were still shouting that they could keep going, but she didn't turn back.

They were all growing stronger, but they didn't rush for revenge.

Later, a map brought by the parrot reconnected them. The three met on a plain. No one asked about gear first, nor did anyone suggest fighting back right away. They just looked at each other for a moment and said, "It's good that we're still here."

## The Evoker in the Woodland Mansion

They eventually decided to go to a Woodland Mansion.

This time it wasn't to take everything, nor to prove they could beat the toughest enemies. Alex drew two retreat routes on the map. Steve was in charge of high-ground scouting, and Pangpang was responsible for holding the line under pressure. The three pets also had their stations: the Little Shadow Dragon for interference, the Mini Wither for suppression, and the parrot for early warning and vocal mimicry.

After entering the mansion, illusions quickly appeared.

At the end of the hallway stood three identical figures, and footsteps echoed from the walls simultaneously. Pangpang held his shield at the front line but didn't rush out immediately. Steve climbed onto the wooden beams, observing the shadows on the ground from above. Alex ignored the most conspicuous target; instead, she stared at a patch of the floor where the light didn't reach.

"The real target wouldn't leave three kinds of footprints," she said.

The parrot mimicked enemy voices from the other side, pretending to order everyone to the left. The Evoker's clones moved accordingly, but the real body remained stationary. Alex raised her hand to point out its position. Steve dropped from the wooden beam, and this time, Pangpang waited until he had firm footing before swinging his mace.

When the battle concluded, none of the three spoke immediately.

Inside the chest were three Totems of Undying.

Pangpang picked up one, Steve grabbed the second, and Alex placed the third into her inventory. Each of them had one; no one had to fight for it.

They sat around the chest for a while. The Little Shadow Dragon rested by Steve's knees, the Mini Wither floated by Alex's shoulder, and the parrot gently pecked at Pangpang's cuff.

Pangpang said, "No one is writing self-reflections this time, right?"

Alex replied, "We'll see if you hide anything when we get back."

## Back to Camp First

After returning from the Woodland Mansion, for the first time, they didn't immediately look for their next objective.

Steve patched the roof, Alex reorganized the warehouse, and Pangpang sat zoning out by the fire with the parrot on his shoulder. The Little Shadow Dragon slept at Steve's feet, and the Mini Wither floated near the storage racks, occasionally knocking off a potion.

They hung up their broken armor, divided the remaining bread into portions, and put the enchanted books back into the chest they had brought from the blacksmith. The extra piece of obsidian still sat in the middle of the table, untouched by anyone.

Pangpang looked at it and said, "Why is this thing still here?"

Steve said, "Because we haven't decided yet."

Alex said, "Or maybe because you've finally realized that not everything has to be dealt with on the same day."

That night, they didn't discuss their next battle. Pangpang sat hugging his knees by the fire. The parrot stayed quiet for a long time before finally whispering, "My armor is expensive."

Pangpang stroked its feathers and said, "I know, I know."

## The Final Strike of the Ender Dragon

They went to the End anyway.

When they first set out, Pangpang had already mentioned this. They needed to mine obsidian, build a portal, and find the Ender Dragon. Too many detours happened along the way; many times they thought if they just went a little further, they could forget their initial goal.

But the End Portal was always there.

They brought their best gear, the three Totems of Undying, and plenty of potions. The Little Shadow Dragon followed Steve, the Mini Wither floated beside Alex, and the parrot stood on Pangpang's shoulder. Pangpang checked his shield, checked his bread, and finally double-checked the straps of his backpack.

"Can't fall into the lava again this time," Steve said.

Pangpang replied, "That was a long time ago."

Alex looked at him: "By your standards, that was yesterday."

When the Ender Dragon dove down from the black sky, Pangpang didn't rush to the very front like before. He waited for Alex to determine the direction, then for Steve to find high ground, and only then did he raise his shield.

The battle raged on much longer than they had anticipated. The Ender Dragon destroyed several obsidian pillars, and purple dragon's breath spread across the ground. Steve used his elytra to fly from one pillar to another, while Alex constantly reminded Pangpang to retreat. The Little Shadow Dragon and Mini Wither provided aerial interference, and the parrot kept yelling, "Don't let go!"

Pangpang heard it, yet he still took a step forward during the dragon's final dive.

That step wasn't reckless. He saw Steve cornered by dragon's breath against a pillar, saw Alex drawing her bow, and saw the dragon's wings folding in. He calculated that he could block this hit with his shield, buying the two of them time to attack.

His calculation was wrong.

The dragon's breath hit the shield, sending Pangpang flying backward. His backpack, bread, and shield all flew out of his hands simultaneously, and the white light of the respawn point flared up behind him. Steve reached out to grab him, and Alex took a step forward, but the distance was too great.

That time, Pangpang was truly killed by the Ender Dragon.

No exaggerations, no pre-prepared jokes. The death message popped up, Pangpang returned to the respawn point, and his items scattered across the black floor of the End. The parrot circled once in the air, and for the first time, it didn't say, "My armor is expensive."

Steve and Alex stood their ground and followed through with the final attack.

When the Ender Dragon fell, the entire sky flashed. Alex picked up Pangpang's backpack, and Steve picked up the bread that had fallen on the ground. They didn't describe this victory as a beautiful comeback, nor did they turn Pangpang's death into a punchline.

They simply returned to the respawn point.

Pangpang was sitting on the edge of his bed, staring blankly at his palms. Seeing the two of them walk over, his first words were: "Where's my bread?"

Steve handed him the bread.

Pangpang took a bite and asked, "Did we beat the dragon?"

Alex said, "We beat it."

Pangpang nodded. After a while, he added, "So, did I count as the frontline this time?"

Steve said, "You did."

"Was it put on the record?"

Alex opened her ledger and wrote a line on the very last page: Pangpang, who was once killed by the Ender Dragon, and later returned to the respawn point. No one was left behind in the battle.

Pangpang leaned over, took a look, and asked, "Why write that I later returned to the respawn point?"

Alex closed the ledger: "Because the story isn't over yet."

When they returned to camp, the extra piece of obsidian was still sitting in the middle of the table.

Pangpang broke the bread into three pieces. Steve took one, Alex took one, and the final piece was left for Pangpang. The parrot stood on the corner of the table, the Mini Wither floated under the lamp, and the Little Shadow Dragon rested at Steve's feet. No one was in a rush to grab the piece of obsidian, and no one asked where they were going next.

The fire burned for a while before Pangpang suddenly spoke up: "How about we start over again tomorrow?"

Alex said, "We could."

Steve said, "But put the bread back first, don't hide it again."

Pangpang swallowed the last bite of bread and nodded earnestly.

Then, while the two weren't paying attention, he slipped a piece of lapis lazuli into his pocket.

The parrot immediately squawked: "Report first!"

Pangpang closed his eyes, knowing that tomorrow's story already had a beginning.
